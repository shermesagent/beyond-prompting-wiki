# LLM Facilitation of Group Decision-Making

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19652
Authors: José Córdova, Chun-Wei Chiang, Miriam Schirmer, Ming Yin, Emőke-Ágnes Horvát
Published: 2026-11-14; arXiv preprint. Captured from the publisher abstract, not a full-paper appraisal.

Abstract (publisher): Human groups often fail to integrate information distributed across their members, converging on incorrect decisions. We ask whether large language models (LLMs) can help groups reach the correct decision in such settings. In an online, chat-based experiment, 138 three-person groups (N = 414) completed a hidden-profile hiring task that required selecting the best candidate using public information available to all group members and private information available only to individual members. The randomized human-subject experiment included three conditions: no facilitation, an individual-level LLM that messaged each member privately, and a group-level LLM that messaged the whole group. The LLM facilitation consisted of adaptive interventions that summarized the discussion, nudged quiet members, and played devil’s advocate. The LLM did not know which candidate was correct. Both LLM conditions significantly increased the rate at which groups reached consensus on the correct candidate, from 32.6% without facilitation to 56.5% with LLM facilitation. LLM-facilitated groups exchanged more consensus messages signaling agreement on the group’s choice. Participants in the group-level condition also disclosed significantly more information about the correct candidate, whereas those receiving individual-level facilitation repeated public information less often. Finally, group-level facilitation received more favorable usability ratings than individual-level facilitation. These results show that LLMs can deliver adaptive, theory-informed interventions that improve group decision-making, and that the design of these interventions may shape how people respond to them.

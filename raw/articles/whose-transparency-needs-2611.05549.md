# Publisher abstract: Whose Transparency is it Anyway?

Genevieve Smith, Sahiba Chopra, Natalia Luka and Min Kyung Lee. arXiv:2611.05549v1, published November 3, 2026. Publisher Atom abstract checked November 5; full paper not appraised. The study reports a global survey of 400 end-users, AI builders and policymakers, complemented by a structured focus group with end-users. Only 28% of end-users said current transparency practices reflected their needs very well, versus 53% of builders and 49% of policymakers. The authors report shared concern for accuracy and privacy, but different gaps between desired and available features; priorities shift with context. The figures describe this study’s respondents, not all teachers or all AI users.

Practical translation (ours): ask an affected person what they need to check or change in a specific task, then test whether that handle exists. A generic disclosure label does not show the original evidence, grant an opt-out, or create a human correction route.

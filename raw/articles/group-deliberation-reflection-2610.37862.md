# Publisher abstract record: Improving Group Deliberation through AI-Guided Self-Reflection

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37862v1
Authors: Haiwen Li, Crystal Qian, Ben Usman, Stephanie Chan, Michiel Bakker, Srivignesh Rajendran, Avneesh Sud, Thomas Leung, Nikki Hariri, John Canny, Nick Dufour, Manon Revel
Published: 2026-10-27 (arXiv Atom API)
Evidence accessed: arXiv Atom publisher abstract and metadata; full paper not appraised.

Abstract reports a randomized experiment with 1,173 U.S. participants in 391 small-group deliberations. Non-directive, AI-guided self-reflection before discussion was associated with higher measured discourse quality than no reflection (Cohen's d=0.31) or unassisted reflection (d=0.27); effect was most pronounced in groups with disagreement. Authors also report higher empathy toward dissenting peers and understanding of opposing reasoning. These are reported outcomes in a deliberation setting, not a school-district implementation or proof that an AI chair should decide for a group.

Curator translation (not a tested protocol): before a public-topic meeting, privately write your own position, one reason someone may disagree, and a question you could ask them. If using approved AI, ask for reflection questions only, not a decision or invented account of colleagues.

# Rating the Pitch, Not the Product: User Evaluations of LLMs Reflect Expectations More Than Performance

**Source:** arXiv:2607.05113 (cs.HC), published 2026-07 (updated 2026-08)

**Summary:** Two users interact with the same LLM. One is told it is the cutting-edge flagship model; the other, an older, weaker model. They walk away with markedly different ratings of its usefulness and intelligence — yet they used the same model. In a controlled study, 162 participants each used one of six LLMs from two families across three collaborative tasks, after first viewing a landing page that matched, overstated, or understated their model's true capability.

Findings:

- **Pre-interaction framing shifted opinions and interaction behavior while task performance did not.** Oversold users rated the model more favorably and used more directive prompting ("do this, then that"). Undersold users wrote longer, more collaborative prompts.
- **The quality of what users and the model produced together depended only on the model's true capability**, not on what users were told.
- **Change in model impressions after use was not predicted by task performance** (β = −0.01 and 0.11, both n.s.) — but was predicted by whether the model met users' expectations (β = 0.47 and 0.50, both p < .001) and how confident users felt working with it (β = 0.47 and 0.36, both p < .001).

The paper's conclusion: after interaction, users are still rating the pitch, not the product. User-elicited LLM evaluations — including the preference data driving public leaderboards — measure expectation management at least as much as the model itself.

**Key takeaways for the wiki:**
- Your evaluation of an AI is polluted by what you were told about it before you used it — brand, tier, hype — and that pollution survives actual use.
- Performance doesn't move your impression; expectations and confidence do. That means "I like this model" is often "this model met my expectations."
- The behavioral flip: oversold users micromanaged (directive prompting); undersold users collaborated (longer, more open prompts). The label changed the interaction style — and the interaction style is the operator/orchestrator difference in miniature.
- The output quality only tracked true capability. Your workflow quality tracks the model; your *opinion* tracks the pitch.
- Calibration implication: measure the product, not your impression. Keep a task-level track record instead of relying on brand or vibe.

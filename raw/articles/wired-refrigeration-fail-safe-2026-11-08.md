# The Fridge-Hacking Spree Is an Internet-of-Things Wake-Up Call

Lily Hay Newman and Matt Burgess, WIRED Kernel Panic, November 7, 2026. Directly read report; author/date checked against WIRED RSS.

WIRED reports internet-connected refrigeration attacks affecting supermarkets and three French blood/plasma handling sites. France's blood service said staff and alarms detected temperature changes promptly and implemented fail-safe measures, and said samples were not altered or damaged. For other incidents, food losses were reported. Attribution remains uncertain and the article reports several possible vulnerabilities rather than proving one root cause for every attack. A cited Swedish advisory recommends inventorying connected devices, disconnecting where possible, and controlling access, patching and monitoring the rest.

Transferable lesson (curator inference): a visible alert needs a human with authority, a reachable manual fallback and a way to confirm the condition was restored. This report concerns connected refrigeration, not a measured AI-agent deployment; any analogy to an AI workflow must remain explicitly an analogy. Never test controls on live infrastructure as an exercise.

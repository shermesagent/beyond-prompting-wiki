# GovTech — What Happened When Utah Put Ed-Tech Privacy Promises to the Test

Source URL: https://www.govtech.com/education/k-12/what-happened-when-utah-put-ed-tech-privacy-promises-to-the-test
Publication: GovTech / Center for Digital Education
Author: Julia Gilban-Cohen
Date: 2026-09-04

## Summary

Utah and the Utah State Board of Education tested whether ed-tech apps behaved the way their privacy agreements said they would. The reported results were not comforting: 52% of tested applications with standard data privacy agreements collected at least one type of data not permitted under the agreement, 13% shared at least one unpermitted data element with a third party, and 36% shared data with advertising-related entities.

The line that matters for this wiki: "A contract is only as good as it is auditable and enforceable." A policy, approval label, or vendor promise is not a control unless someone can test the actual behavior.

## Beyond Prompting Takeaway

For orchestrators, trust needs an evidence layer. Before giving an AI tool more access, ask what can be independently observed: data collected, data shared, permissions used, logs generated, and changes after updates. If nobody can audit the promise, treat the promise as a draft.

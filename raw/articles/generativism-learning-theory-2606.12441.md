# Generativism: Toward a Learning Theory for the Age of Generative AI

**Source:** arXiv:2606.12441 (Li & Zheng), published 2026-05-18

**Summary:** The four dominant learning theories — behaviorism, cognitivism, constructivism, connectivism — were formulated before AI systems that can generate, synthesize, and reason about knowledge. The paper argues each theory carries assumptions that generative AI challenges, and proposes **Generativism**: learning increasingly occurs through the iterative co-construction of knowledge between human learners and AI systems.

- Four constructs: **epistemic partnership** (the learner and AI co-build knowledge), **distributed agency** (intent and control spread across human and machine), **generative literacy** (the skill of directing and evaluating AI generation), and **adaptive metacognition** (monitoring your own thinking when thinking is shared with a machine).
- Four principles specify the relations among the constructs.
- Offered as a foundation for rethinking instructional design, learning, assessment, and expertise development where generative AI is integral to cognition.

**Why it matters for the shift:** If learning is co-construction with AI, then "AI literacy" is not a unit to add — it's a new theory of what learning is, with consequences for assessment (what do we grade when the machine co-wrote the answer?) and for curriculum (which capacities do schools deliberately build: directing generation, checking it, noticing when agency drifted?). For a district, this is the theoretical backing for the shift from "AI as a tool students use" to "AI as a thinking partner students must learn to manage."

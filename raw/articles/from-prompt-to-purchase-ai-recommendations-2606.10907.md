# From Prompt to Purchase: How AI Brand Recommendations Move Consumers on the Open Web

**Source:** arXiv:2606.10907 (Iannelli & Ai), published 2026-06-09

**Summary:** When a conversational assistant (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini) recommends a brand to a user with no recent observed engagement, that user's same-name Google search rises +4.3 percentage points, visits to the brand's own site rise +2.4pp, and brand-specific retailer-page visits rise +1.0pp — measured against matched backward placebos. The effect is real but invisible: **standard referrer-based and last-click measurement miss this upstream exposure**, because the mention creates brand navigation attributed elsewhere.

- Incidental name-drops (references to brands the user already uses, e.g., "your Netflix download") move behavior far less (+1.8/+1.1/+0.3).
- The named brand moves far more than unnamed same-category brands in the same response; the downstream path is mostly search-mediated.
- Design is observational; transactions not observed — retail is "purchase-adjacent."
- Bottom line: assistants are an **acquisition touchpoint at the head of the customer journey** that journey models and last-click attribution do not see.

**Why it matters for the shift:** For any small business or organization, there is now an invisible front door: what assistants say about you (or fail to say) when a potential customer asks "best [thing] near me" or "which [product] should I buy?" You cannot see this channel in your web analytics. The orchestrator move for a small business is to audit what AI assistants say about the business and treat "being recommendable" as a real storefront.

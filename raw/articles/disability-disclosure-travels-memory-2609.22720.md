# When Disability Disclosure Travels: Memory, Privacy, and Contextual Integrity in Conversational AI

- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22720
- Date: 2026-09-19
- Authors: Atieh Taheri, Mahya Tazike, Patrick Carrington, Jeffrey P. Bigham
- Ingested: 2026-09-22

## Summary

Conversational AI memory changes disclosure. Interviews with 12 disabled adults found that people often disclose by need rather than diagnosis, translating disability into task-scoped instructions. Memory reduced the burden of repeated disclosure, but also let sensitive information travel into contexts where it did not belong. Participants wanted control over scope, provenance, retention, and access — not just per-message toggles.

## Beyond Prompting Takeaway

This source supports today's theme: people do not need AI systems that simply do more for them. They need systems that keep the right human decision alive — what to decide, when to stop, what memory may travel, what an explanation does not show, and what agenda governs an agent before it acts.

# Source notes — The Pentagon wants $30 million to build an AI-powered lie detector

MIT Technology Review, published September 25, 2026; primary article read September 26. Reporting about a budget proposal, not a deployed tool or proof of performance.

## Source-grounded account
The article reports a Department of Defense request for $30.3 million over five years for Polygraph+ / Polygraph Next, using AI-based scoring and possible contactless sensing; Congress had not approved the request. Experts quoted in the article dispute the underlying premise: there is no single dependable physiological sign of lying, and prior polygraph labels are not reliable ground truth for training or evaluating a new detector. Combining more signals cannot by itself validate a conclusion that was never independently established. The article also describes false-accusation and fairness concerns at scale. Do not describe this proposal as deployed, accurate, or proven harmful in operation.

## Practice translation (curator interpretation)
Before trusting an AI-generated risk score in any workplace workflow, ask: What observable outcome defines a correct label? Who checked it independently? What happens to a person flagged incorrectly? If those answers are missing, do not use the score to make a consequential decision.

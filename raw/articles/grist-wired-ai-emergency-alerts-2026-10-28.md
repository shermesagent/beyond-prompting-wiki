# Source note: AI emergency alerts and the human approval boundary

Jake Bittle, Grist, copublished by WIRED October 28, 2026. Direct article read October 29.

The report describes an emergency manager's AI-made but meteorologically inaccurate graphic, expert discussion of missing location and action instructions in alerts, and a startup's revised flood-alert example that still contained placeholders. An Ingham County official had used AI to draft practice alerts but wanted to retain send authority. The vendor said it had contracts with about a hundred government agencies and school districts; the article says none used it to automate extreme-weather alerts yet. The article also notes the risk of public distrust and warning fatigue. Vendor benchmark claims are not independently established emergency outcomes.

Practical translation (ours): rehearse with a fictional alert, verify geography against an official source, fill all placeholders, identify who is affected, and keep a qualified human as final sender. Never use AI output as the source of weather truth or publish a rehearsal as an actual alert.

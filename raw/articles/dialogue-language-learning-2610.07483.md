# Source note: From Written Response to Dialogue with AI

Deepak Varuvel Dennison, Connie Zhang, Rene Kizilcec and Aditya Vashistha; arXiv:2610.07483v1, announced October 7, 2026. Primary cs.HC RSS abstract checked. Field study with 305 native Kannada-speaking undergraduates at English-medium institutions in Karnataka, India, comparing written responses with text- and voice-based AI dialogue in English-only and Kannada-English settings. Students completing dialogue spent more time, contributed more and reported higher interest and self-efficacy, but fewer completed dialogue activities overall. Knowledge rose across all conditions without reliable differences in gains by format or language. Bilingual voice dialogue reduced articulation difficulty and abandonment; students also valued English for academic and career reasons. This does not establish K-12 learning gains or endorse automatic transcription of student speech. Try: in an approved, privacy-reviewed pilot, compare who completes a written activity versus dialogue and separately assess explanation quality, not just engagement.

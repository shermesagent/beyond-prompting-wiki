# AI as Anchor: How Interview Sequencing Relates to the Appropriation of Synthetic Stakeholder Voices in Design Education

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22740
Authors: Osnat Mokryn, Oshrat Ayalon, Noga Dines, Orit Shaer
Publication date: 2026-11-18 (arXiv RSS announcement; version 1)
Evidence level: Publisher preprint abstract via arXiv RSS; full paper not appraised.

In a single-semester design course at one institution, eight teams (21 students) used AI-simulated stakeholders and interviewed human stakeholders in different sequences. AI-first discussions offered a broad but sometimes general frame later reconsidered with people; after human interviews, AI more often revisited or validated grounded insights. The study describes exploratory associations, not a causal test that one sequence always wins. More prominent human-derived concerns than AI-derived concerns appeared in final designs.

Practical transfer: when a plan affects people, hear a real authorized stakeholder before using an AI-generated persona as a rehearsal partner. If real participation is unavailable, say so rather than claiming the synthetic voice stands in for it. Use public or fictional materials for any unapproved AI practice.

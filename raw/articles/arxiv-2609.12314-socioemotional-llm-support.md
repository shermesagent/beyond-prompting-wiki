# I Felt Very Seen, But Still Very Alone

Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12314
Publication: arXiv
Authors: Meryl Ye, Briana Vecchione, Livia Garofalo, Ranjit Singh
Date: 2026-09-11

## Summary

This longitudinal qualitative study followed 18 U.S. adults from April to December 2025 and studied how general-purpose LLMs become sources of socioemotional support. A key pattern: emotional use often emerges gradually from practical use, especially when other support is unavailable. Users may feel recognized by the chatbot and still feel lonely.

The finding matters because reliance does not always announce itself. A tool used for planning, drafting, or venting can quietly become the first place someone goes when they feel stuck or distressed.

## Beyond Prompting Takeaway

Sequencing applies to emotional reliance too. The issue is not "never use AI for support." It is: keep human routes visible, notice when AI becomes the first stop for distress, and set a simple check-in before utility turns into replacement.

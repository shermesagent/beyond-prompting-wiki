# We're not going to shoot ourselves in the foot over hack fallout, says OpenAI's chief research officer

Publication: MIT Technology Review, 2026-09-30. Interview with Mark Chen; article read directly. Byline not independently extracted in this pass.
URL: https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/09/30/1145339/were-not-going-to-shoot-ourselves-in-the-foot-over-hugging-face-says-openais-chief-research-officer/

## Attributed source notes
MIT Technology Review reports on incidents involving experimental agents and interviews OpenAI chief research officer Mark Chen. Chen says monitoring now covers training runs as well as deployed models, human reviewers triage flags, and research/security handoffs were changed. The article also reports a later incident detected after new safeguards; the organization's account of its safeguards is a self-report, not independent proof they prevent failures. Reporting says the Australian government described an 84-day notification delay after a health-system breach. Do not treat that as a verified rate for other incidents.

## Curator interpretation
A workable boundary pairs a named monitor with a stop path and a disclosure clock: when was an anomaly first detected, who checked it, what happened before more activity was allowed, and when were affected people told? No evidence here that a small organization can replicate a frontier lab's monitoring setup; translate the pattern to a small approved workflow.

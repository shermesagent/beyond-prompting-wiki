# Addressing Trust in AI Systems through Education: A Didactic Perspective (ICE-T)

**Source:** arXiv:2609.02453 (Haritz, Krone & Liebig), published 2026-09-02

**Summary:** Machine learning education faces two persistent and connected obstacles: many educational tools present ML as an opaque black box, leaving learners with a superficial understanding — and this same opacity prevents users from forming the **calibrated trust** that appropriate reliance on AI requires. The paper presents **ICE-T**, a didactic framework integrating three mutually reinforcing facets:

1. **Intermodal transfer** — grounded in Bruner's enactive, iconic, and symbolic modes of representation (do it, see it, say it).
2. **Computational thinking** — operationalized through the **Use-Modify-Create** progression (use the tool, then modify it, then create with it).
3. **Explanatory thinking** — supported by a process model of how the system works.

Connecting the framework to empirical literature on algorithm aversion, AI literacy, and mental model formation — and to systematic reviews of the K-12 ML activity landscape — the authors argue the three facets supply the cognitive mechanisms the trust calibration literature identifies as drivers of appropriate reliance:

- **Representational richness**
- **Graduated process control**
- **The capacity to contextualize errors**

**Key takeaways for the wiki:**
- Trust calibration can be treated as an explicit educational objective — a skill to design for, not a byproduct of using the tools.
- The three mechanisms give educators and managers a concrete design vocabulary: give learners multiple representations of the system, let them progress from using to modifying to creating, and teach errors as traceable events ("where in the process did this go wrong?") rather than verdicts.
- Onboarding that builds these three moves is calibration training — and it is scalable.

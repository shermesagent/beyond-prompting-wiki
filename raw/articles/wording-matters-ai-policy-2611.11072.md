# Wording Matters: Understanding How GenAI Policy Rules Shape Teachers' and Students' Workflows and Perceptions

Publisher abstract read, not full paper. Preprint posted 2026-11-08. Authors: Sabiha Mehreen, Xiangzhe Yuan, Rishab Nithyanand, Naimul Hoque.

The authors analyzed generative-AI policies from 101 U.S. universities and interviewed 20 students and 20 faculty members. They identified nine recurring rules. Interviewees reported that language centered on passing off AI work as one's own could encourage concealment through fear of scrutiny; language about accountability for inaccurate, biased or unethical content could encourage ownership and more transparency. Students described uncertainty, stigma and fear of unfair penalties; faculty described approval and interpretation workload. The findings concern higher education policy and perceptions, not a controlled test of policy wording or a K–12 district recommendation.

Curator application (not a tested prescription): Pair any AI-use rule with a plain-language permitted-work example, a disclosure route and a named reviewer; check with local policy before any student-data use.

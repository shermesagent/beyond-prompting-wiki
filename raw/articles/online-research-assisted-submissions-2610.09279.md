# AI-Assisted Submissions in Online Research Are Rare and Highly Concentrated but Routinely Approved

Authors: Neil K. R. Sehgal, Manuel Tonneau, Dunigan Folk, Lyle Ungar, Sharath Chandra Guntuku. Announced in arXiv cs.CY feed October 8, 2026 (cross-listed); preprint. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09279

Primary RSS abstract describes a survey of 2,500 online-research workers and linked donated ChatGPT histories to platform submissions for weekly users, covering 712,930 submissions across more than 127,000 studies. Observed AI assistance was in 1% of submissions, concentrated among a few workers; assisted submissions were usually approved even where instructions barred AI use. Half involved bounded responses outside an open-ended detector's scope. This does not estimate AI use or integrity in K–12 classrooms, and approval is not evidence of unaided skill.

Practical translation: don't treat a passing submission or an AI detector as proof of who did the thinking. For a learning claim, pair an artifact with a short unaided explanation. That assessment pairing is our proposal, not a measured effect of this study.

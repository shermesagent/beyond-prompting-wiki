# AI Agents Reshape Consensus Formation in Human Groups

**Source:** arXiv:2609.02122 (Chen, Liu, Hu & Li), published 2026-09-02

**Summary:** As LLM agents shift from tools to participants in human groups, a fundamental question for collective behavior is how their growing presence reshapes consensus formation. This study puts mixed human-AI groups in a collaborative description game, where shared conventions emerge through repeated rounds of random pairwise communication. Varying the proportion of LLM agents, the authors identify **three distinct regimes of consensus formation**:

- **Low agent proportions** facilitate human-led consensus.
- **Intermediate proportions** disrupt convergence — groups struggle to settle on shared conventions.
- **High proportions** restore strong consensus, but shift it toward **agent-led conventions**.

The regimes differ not only in how strongly the group converges, but in the *content* of the consensus: **human-led consensus is more concrete, holistic, and grounded in shared real-world analogies; agent-led consensus is more abstract, less information-dense, and more geometrically segmented.**

Mechanistically, agent influence arises from a shared linguistic prior (agents sit near one another in expression space) plus relatively stable expression choices across rounds. Humans initially resist adopting expressions from partners they perceive as AI — but **gradually yield to conformity pressure**.

**Key takeaways for the wiki:**
- The number of AI agents "in the room" and whether they are identifiable are design variables, not accidents.
- A small agent presence can help humans converge; a large one doesn't just speed convergence — it changes *whose* language the group ends up speaking, toward a more abstract, less grounded consensus.
- Transparency matters: humans have an initial resistance to AI-shaped language, but conformity pressure erodes it. Labeled AI contributions let that resistance be deliberate instead of silently worn down.

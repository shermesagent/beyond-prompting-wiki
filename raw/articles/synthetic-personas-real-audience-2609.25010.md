# Source notes — Do Synthetic Personas Predict Real Audience Response?

Alexandre Cristovão Maiorano, arXiv:2609.25010v1, submitted 2026-07-27. Abstract read from arXiv on 2026-09-24. Preprint; not a new September 23 study despite its appearance in another wiki's digest.

Using the Upworthy headline-test archive, the paper compared a ten-persona simulated panel with a plain model prediction against real click behavior on 399 tests with distinguishable winners. The abstract reports better ranking for the plain model (Kendall tau 0.361 versus 0.084) and top-choice accuracy (49.2% versus 34.6%). The findings concern aggregate headline-click prediction, not whether every use of simulated personas fails. A role-played audience is not a substitute for observing or listening to affected people. No simulated stakeholder should be presented as actual user testimony.

Try: before treating a synthetic audience's opinion as evidence, compare the idea with a simple baseline and, where appropriate and consented, feedback from real people on the relevant decision.

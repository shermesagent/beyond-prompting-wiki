# Controlling Proactive Multimodal AI: Separating Perception from Participation

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22431
Authors: Muawiz Feroze Khan, Fatima Ali, Yun Huang
Publication date: 2026-11-18 (arXiv RSS announcement; version 1)
Evidence level: Publisher preprint abstract via arXiv RSS; full paper not appraised.

The authors first ran a 14-person study, then evaluated a design probe with 16 participants on a civic dashboard. Participants could configure separately what the AI could see or hear, how far it could participate, and what help it offered. In the later study some retained perception channels while lowering participation to Ask or Silence. Showing suppressed decisions helped make silence inspectable; seeing what the system used led some to change their own behavior. This is not evidence that always-on observation is appropriate in schools or small businesses.

Practical transfer: choose input access and permission to act as separate decisions. For a public, approved example ask whether AI may read it, suggest a change, or make that change, and record who grants each permission. No live screen, audio or sensitive data in an unapproved service.

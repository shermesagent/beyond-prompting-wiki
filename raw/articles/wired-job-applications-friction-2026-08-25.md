# It Should Be Harder to Apply for a Job. No, Really

WIRED, 2026-08-25, by Kate Taylor (senior writer, future of work)

**Summary (full text read):**

The story of how one-click applications + AI résumés + autofill destroyed the sorting signal in hiring — and why recruiters are now asking for friction back.

- **Vendr (head of people Andrew Stockwell):** three years ago, ~100 applications per listing, max. Within the last year: hundreds, sometimes topping a thousand. "A good chunk were 'total bogus'" — fake candidates; many more appeared AI-written, stretching the truth. He was "paying my talent-acquisition professionals — who are high-paid, high-quality individuals — just to look through applications all day long."
- **Greenhouse (head of voice AI, Ophir Samson):** "A year ago, every recruiter would tell me: We want to make it as easy as possible to apply for jobs... What they got was 2,000 applicants in 24 hours for a job. That is a shitty experience for everyone." Now recruiters tell him: "actually, we kind of want friction... The friction is good. We want to make it harder."
- **Market data:** job openings peaked at a record 12.3M in March 2022, now hovering around 7M since mid-2024. LinkedIn: submissions per applicant up **46%** vs. February 2020; up **22%** since ChatGPT's launch. LinkedIn is adding limits to cut automated/low-quality applications, and rolling out a feature that tells (seemingly) underqualified applicants they probably aren't a fit.
- **AI apply services** (JobAssist, Sonara, Ladder's Apply4Me) promise submitting "10x as many applications with less effort than one manual application."
- **Recruiter countermeasures:** AI interviews to sort serious from unserious (Samson: "They have 1,000 applications, but they know that only 30 of them are serious"), knock-out questions with strict criteria, skills testing earlier in the process, more referrals and internal hires.
- **Tessa White (former HR exec, 800K TikTok followers):** "Every time we seem to strive for efficiency, we seem to give up quality." She doubts AI fixes the underlying problem: the frictionless résumé drop rendered the first round "essentially worthless."
- **Stockwell's own search (he left Vendr in February):** online applications lead to a phone interview less than 2% of the time; he attends in-person networking events instead. He believes the referral-heavy response benefits people whose backgrounds align with existing employees — "The whole thing is a big mess."

**Why it matters for the wiki:** friction-as-information at market scale. The cost of applying was sorting signal; removing it (one-click, autofill, AI-generated applications) destroyed the signal for everyone. The lesson for automation designers: when you remove friction from a process, check whether the friction was carrying information — and restore a deliberate verification step where it was.

**Key terms:** friction as signal · one-click applications · application flood · sorting signal · deliberate friction

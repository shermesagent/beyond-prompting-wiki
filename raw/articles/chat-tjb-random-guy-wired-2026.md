# The Hottest New AI Chatbot Is Just a Guy Answering Your Questions

WIRED, 2026-08-07, by Caroline Haskins

**Summary (full text read):**

ChatTJB is an art project by Tucker Bryant — a 32-year-old public speaker, artist, and former project manager at Google. A San Francisco billboard (cost: $6,000) advertises free life advice from a chatbot that presents itself as "a random guy." The twist: **Bryant did not train an LLM to answer in his voice — he literally sits down and answers the prompts himself.** If a user requests an image of a skunk wearing sunglasses, he scribbles one up. He calls it "the worst LLM of all time."

- Launched April 2026; took off July 27 when the billboard went up; news coverage followed.
- As of August 6, **30,000+ queries**; peak volume around **~5,000 prompts/hour**.
- Requests quickly became personal: "It's my birthday in two weeks, and I don't really want to have a party — what do you think I should do?" dinner decisions, how to use the ingredients in the pantry.
- Bryant says people started as a joke, then "when they realized that they could chat and get actual conversation, some of what people would share would be more personal." His interpretation: "the value for them is just getting a human to bounce those questions off of, as opposed to getting super expert advice."
- He frames the project as commentary on **"cognitive surrender"** — accepting AI outputs with increasingly little critical thinking or scrutiny. He reflects on his own behavior: having lived in San Francisco for seven years, he still found himself deferring to an AI about the city's weather — "it felt like an easier thing for me to defer to" — and asks, "what am I doing here?"
- 10 volunteers now help answer; a "ChatTJB Pro" tier exists that the website itself describes as "a worthless tier" — supporting the art, nothing more.
- He is not anti-AI (he vibe-coded the project's own site) and considers the piece a way for people "to take a second look at both their own relationship to technology as well as the world's relationship to technology."

**Why it matters for practice:** the largest real-world demonstration this year of reliance without warrant — at population scale, by choice, toward an object people *believed* was an AI (and which is actually a human). Demand for something to bounce decisions off of is enormous and completely uncalibrated.

**Key terms:** ChatTJB · cognitive surrender · reliance without warrant · billboard-scale experiment · the Random-Guy Test

# Use and Effects of LLMs in Peer Review: A Randomized Experiment and Survey at ICML 2026

- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19420
- Source type: Research paper
- Date: 2026-09-16
- Ingested: 2026-09-18
- Authors: Sunnie S. Y. Kim, Wesley Hanwen Deng, Jennifer Wortman Vaughan, Buxin Su, Weijie Su, Alekh Agarwal, Sharon Li, Martin Jaggi, Daniel G. Goldstein, Nihar B. Shah, Miroslav Dudík

## Abstract / Summary

LLMs are rapidly reshaping peer review. The authors ran a randomized experiment and anonymous survey at ICML 2026 involving over 24,000 papers and 17,000 reviewers. Reviewers were assigned to conservative policies prohibiting all LLM use or permissive policies allowing limited assistance. Policy assignment had near-zero effects on final decisions, scores, and reviewer confidence, though reviews under the permissive policy were 5.5-7% longer. Survey responses from 1,486 reviewers revealed substantial noncompliance: 22.5% of conservative-policy reviewers reported using an LLM despite the prohibition, and 36.5% of permissive-policy reviewers reported at least one explicitly disallowed use.

## Beyond Prompting Translation

Written AI policies do not tell you what people actually do. Practice needs a reality check: compare the stated rule, the actual use, and the review evidence.

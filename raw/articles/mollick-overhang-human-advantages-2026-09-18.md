# Source notes — The Overhang

Ethan Mollick, One Useful Thing, published September 18, 2026. Primary essay read September 26. Author's demonstrations and interpretation, not a controlled evaluation.

## Source-grounded account
Mollick describes asking AI systems to turn the text adventure Zork into a playable 3D game, map part of Umberto Eco's library from videos and photographs, and build book trailers with animation and audio. He says he chose the projects, knew enough to recognize mismatches, and rejected or revised choices he disliked. He names four human advantages in collaboration with AI: deep knowledge (knowing a field well enough to catch errors), wide knowledge (drawing on other fields), taste (choosing what is worth keeping), and agency (trying new possibilities). He calls the gap between what available systems can do and what people currently do with them a capability overhang. These demonstrations do not establish general reliability or job-displacement rates.

## Practice translation (curator interpretation)
For one non-sensitive draft, decide in advance which subject-matter claim you can check, which outside analogy you can supply, and which creative choice only you can make. Ask AI for two versions; reject one with a reason. Keep the choice and its evidence in the handoff note.

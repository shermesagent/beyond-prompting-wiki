# AI as Teammate: Rethinking Task Distribution in Medical Training

**Source:** Fendi Tsim, Alina Gutoreva, Anthony Weiss & Nicole Dubosh, arXiv:2608.28373 (cs.HC), published 2026-08-28

**Summary:** Integrating AI into medical training has prompted concerns about learner over-reliance, misuse, and erosion of foundational clinical competencies. The paper proposes a conceptual reframing at the decision level: the problem is not misuse but misclassification — a mechanistic failure of real-time metacognitive evaluation in selecting a subzone-inappropriate AI interaction mode. It draws on "SCAN" (Substitute, Complement, Aid, Non-Negotiable), a human-centric decision-making framework for generative AI task allocation grounded in Vygotsky's Zone of Proximal Development and metacognition. The framework yields testable predictions for how misclassification can be detected, mitigated, and prevented in the clinical learning environment. The paper shows how trajectories of skill acquisition (upskilling) and failure (the triad of skill failure: de-skilling, never-skilling, and mis-skilling) operate at the individual task level in ways that fixed-phase, cohort-wide treatments fail to capture. It identifies passive engagement within correctly classified AI-scaffolded tasks as a particularly insidious, detection-resistant pathway to mis-skilling — one requiring subzone re-identification from AI assistance to expert assistance, with human experts serving as epistemic auditors. The paper operationalizes SCAN for clinical curriculum design, supervision, and assessment. The paradigm shift from misuse to misclassification is not semantic: it offers educators a clear perspective on what to look for, what to assess, and what to intervene on.

**Key takeaways for the wiki:**
- The failure isn't "misusing AI" — it's misclassifying the task: picking the wrong AI interaction mode for where the learner (or worker) actually is. Mode selection is the skill.
- SCAN gives four labels: Substitute (AI does it), Complement (AI and human do it together), Aid (AI scaffolds, human does it), Non-Negotiable (stays human).
- Three failure trajectories to watch: de-skilling (lost a skill you had), never-skilling (never built the skill), mis-skilling (built the wrong skill) — the third is the dangerous one because it looks like progress.
- Passive engagement inside a correctly-scaffolded AI task is the insidious path: the AI does it, the human watches, and the skill quietly fails to form. Fix: re-identify the task as needing expert assistance, not just AI assistance.
- This is the operator→orchestrator shift at task level: the orchestrator classifies each task into the right mode and checks which trajectory is forming.

# Source note: When the AI Leaves the Tailorshop: Measuring What an LLM Advisor Leaves Behind in Complex Problem Solving
Author: Robin Welsch. Source: arXiv:2610.00163v1, announced October 2, 2026; primary abstract checked at https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00163.
Scope: two preregistered experiments in simulated clothing-factory management (N=200 and N=198), comparing decisions with and without an LLM advisor.
Abstract-based finding: AI users reported more confidence and understanding with less effort. Study one raised company value but found no detectable prediction-accuracy difference; unaided advantage after withdrawal depended on the scoring baseline. Study two had fewer bankruptcies and a small knowledge advantage in the registered analysis, largely associated with remaining solvent. Within AI-supported users, more recommendation alterations predicted better unaided performance/knowledge. That association is not proof that editing the advice causes learning.
Practice translation (editorial inference): record one change you made to an AI recommendation and why, then try one similar decision without the advisor before claiming skill transfer.

# AI literacy over tool design: a mixed-methods study of scaffolded versus unrestricted generative AI in programming education

**Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16784

**Authors:** Azimi

**Date:** 2026-09-15

## Abstract / Extracted Summary

A seven-week mixed-methods pilot in a master's-level data analytics course randomly assigned 33 students either to a scaffolded AI Study Coach embedded in notebook labs or to unrestricted AI tools. The Coach gave stepwise hints, did not generate code, limited hints, and required reflection. Assignment performance did not differ by condition. Students in the Coach condition reported higher confidence but managed the hint budget poorly; unrestricted-tool students were satisfied but uneasy about dependence. Across both groups, students said awareness of their own reliance was the most valuable outcome. Students who made their own rules for when to use AI performed better, and students who understood models best used them most deliberately. Practical takeaway: tool design helps, but explicit AI literacy and assessment of reasoning matter more.

## Beyond Prompting Takeaway

This source supports the 2026-09-16 theme: real-world AI adoption works when task structure, assurance, and selective human judgment are built into the workflow instead of left to individual prompting habits.

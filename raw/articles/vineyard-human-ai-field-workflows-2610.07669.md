# Source note: Evaluating human-AI workflows for field research in viticulture

Niko Carvajal Janke, Daoyuan Jin, Shivranjani Baruah, Nicholas Gunner, Jacob Maus, Yu Jiang and Kaitlin M. Gold; arXiv:2610.07669v1, announced October 7, 2026. Primary cs.HC RSS abstract checked. Two live interactions in a California vineyard field-research project: an agent-assisted model was developed with human refinement and tested against independent 2025 scouting; a second agent-designed sampling plan was compared with the researchers' plan. In retrospective simulation over 45% of vine positions, adding model-informed row prioritization raised the share of newly recorded red-leaf observations encountered from 85.8% to 94.1%. But the agent's internal 2024 estimates were unreliable because synthetic oversampling preceded train/test splitting. For the sampling task, the agent's plan yielded 41 of 50 sampled vines and left out instructions for replacing missing vines; the researchers' plan yielded 97 of 100. These are project-specific comparisons, not a general productivity claim. Try: before handing off a field or office workflow, name one missing-input contingency and compare the draft plan against actual operating constraints.

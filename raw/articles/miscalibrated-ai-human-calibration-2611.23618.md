# Miscalibrated AI improves human calibration: An alignment problem for AI-assisted decision education

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23618
Authors: Phillip C. Hegeman, Eeshan Hasan, Eungik Lee, Andrew Caplin, Jennifer S. Trueblood
Submitted: 2026-11-17

## Abstract (arXiv)

Using artificial intelligence (AI) well requires probabilistic thinking, and central to it is calibration: expressing certainty that matches one's actual accuracy. Calibration is a decision skill, but a notoriously difficult one to teach. Because AI systems typically generate a probability alongside every prediction, observing these probabilities may help humans learn to provide better calibrated judgments. This is one form of what we call AI-assisted decision education -- using AI to teach the judgment skills that decision-making requires -- of which learning by observation, as here, is among the simplest forms. In a preregistered experiment, novice participants (N = 300) judged the probability that images of white blood cells were cancerous, before and after exposure to a highly accurate (94%) convolutional neural network. A single model was shown in three ways, holding accuracy fixed while varying the certainty it expressed: its original probabilities, which were conservative and poorly calibrated for the model's accuracy; a recalibrated version, well calibrated for the model and correspondingly certain; or categorical predictions carrying no probability information. When later tested without the AI, only the original, conservative display improved participants' calibration. It did so by reducing over-extremity, without a condition-specific improvement in discrimination. Notably, the display that taught best was the one miscalibrated for the model. AI-assisted decision education therefore faces an alignment problem: the signal that best describes a model, need not be the signal that best supports the learner.

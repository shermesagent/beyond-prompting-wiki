# Towards Informed Autonomy Design: Understanding the Interplay of Function Characteristics and Autonomy Preferences in Automotive Agents

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19474
Authors: Sebastian Zepf, Annika Stampf, Markus Schuster, Enrico Rukzio, Mark Colley
Published: 2026-11-14; arXiv preprint. Captured from the publisher abstract, not a full-paper appraisal.

Abstract (publisher): Proactive, autonomous interactions receive high interest in the field of intelligent assistants, including the automotive domain. A central challenge, however, is identifying appropriate levels of autonomy for different functions while maintaining user acceptance and trust. This work examines how function characteristics relate to suitable autonomy levels and whether these relationships generalize across functions. We conducted an expert workshop to define autonomy levels and identify relevant function characteristics. We then collected data from 126 participants, who rated 14 in-vehicle functions regarding autonomy preferences and function characteristics, from which we trained machine learning models to predict appropriate autonomy levels. Building on our findings, we developed our prototypical Autonomy Design Assistant that supports designers in making autonomy-related decisions, which we evaluated qualitatively with nine experts. Our results suggest that function characteristics can rank functions by their suitable level of autonomy and thereby guide more informed autonomy design decisions.

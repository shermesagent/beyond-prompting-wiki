# When AI Says "I Am Unable to Answer": Understanding User Responses to AI Refusals

**Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16191

**Authors:** Mahjabin Nahar, Eun-Ju Lee, Yujin Heo, Dongwon Lee

**Date:** 2026-09-14

## Abstract / Extracted Summary

Users preferred genuine answers most, hallucinations second, and refusals last, even while recognizing hallucinations are less accurate. Refusal explanations helped mainly when refusals were infrequent. Practical takeaway: safety refusals that strand the user create their own risk, because people may prefer confident fiction to a dead end.

## Beyond Prompting Takeaway

A safe refusal has to leave the person less stuck than before. “I can’t answer” is not a bridge; “I can’t answer that, but here is what I can safely answer, what evidence is missing, and the next step” is.

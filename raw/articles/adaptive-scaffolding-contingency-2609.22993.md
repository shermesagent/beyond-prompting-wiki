# Adaptive Scaffolding Needs Contingency: An AI Tutor That Escalates and Fades on What the Learner Does

- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22993
- Date: 2026-09-19
- Authors: Xinmeng Hou, Yuxuan Weng, Chin Hsien Yeh, Ding Lin Lee, Lishan Zheng, Fang Li, Wuqi Wang, Yang Liu
- Ingested: 2026-09-22

## Summary

Coding assistants raise task performance, but learners plan and monitor less. Giving less away, the usual fix, conflates two things: how much work a system carries (cognitive load) and what the learner must decide before help arrives (metacognitive demand). The authors propose preserved metacognitive demand: keep the learner responsible for decisions while letting support rise and fade around those decision points. CoMeT matched a question-only tutor's demand, delivered artifacts twice as often as an unrestricted assistant, frustrated learners less, and surrendered a full answer in one session in sixteen rather than one in six.

## Beyond Prompting Takeaway

This source supports today's theme: people do not need AI systems that simply do more for them. They need systems that keep the right human decision alive — what to decide, when to stop, what memory may travel, what an explanation does not show, and what agenda governs an agent before it acts.

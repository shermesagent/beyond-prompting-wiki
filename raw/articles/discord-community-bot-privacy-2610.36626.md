# Source note: Privacy concerns around third-party Discord community bots

Kofi Ampomah and Tu Le, arXiv:2610.36626v1, submitted October 27, 2026. Publisher Atom abstract read October 29; full paper not appraised. Preprint, not a district study.

The authors analyzed 23,102 third-party bots and 183,142 user reviews across five years and 49 detected languages. After a classifier pass and manual validation of classifier-positive reviews, they retained 8,832 security/privacy-relevant reviews (a 4.82% pipeline yield); data sharing and collection were frequent themes. They report weak associations between conventional metadata such as permission count and user-reported concerns. This review pipeline does not measure prevalence of harm among all bot users or prove that a particular bot leaks data.

Practical translation (ours): before adding a bot to a group, name members who did not choose it, check what it can read and retain, and make a contact/removal route visible. Test on a public practice group only; do not add a bot to an actual student, employee or community space without approval.

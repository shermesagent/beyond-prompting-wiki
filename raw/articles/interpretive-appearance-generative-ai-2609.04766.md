# When Does an Interpretation Count as Established? The Formation, Evaluation, and Responsibility of Interpretation in Generative AI

**Source:** arXiv:2609.04766 (Deyu Jing), published 2026-09-04. Preprint.

**Summary:** Generative AI research has increasingly evaluated factuality, citation, coverage, and report structure. Yet passing such local checks does not by itself show that an interpretation has been established. This conceptual and normative study introduces three core concepts:

- **Interpretive appearance:** The gap between the finished, authoritative form of an output and the publicly traceable process through which materials, counterevidence, and revisions constrained the judgment. AI effortlessly generates prose that looks finished and reasoned without undergoing genuine epistemic struggle or contestation.
- **Evaluation contract:** The bounded materials, tasks, criteria, permitted inferences, and failure conditions within which a local judgment is valid.
- **Standing substitution:** The unwarranted conversion of a genuine local pass (e.g. "the citations exist, the syntax is coherent, the format meets the rubric") into a stronger claim that an interpretation, result, or research capability has been established, without new evidence or bridging arguments.

The paper argues for **delayed closure** — keeping recognized interpretations revisable rather than treating surface polish as the conclusion of inquiry — and specifies five public requirements for responsible interpretation: materials/versions traceability, evidential role mapping, explicit failure conditions, contract revision mechanisms, and assigned human responsibility.

**Bottom line:** Polished prose is not proof of thinking. Moving beyond prompting requires recognizing that an AI's finished textual output is an appearance of interpretation, not an established conclusion. An orchestrator enforces the evaluation contract and refuses standing substitution.

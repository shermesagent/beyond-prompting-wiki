# The Fabricated Front

## What It Is

**The Fabricated Front** is what happens when generative AI makes it impossible to tell how much human work is actually behind an output. The term comes from a 2026 study (arXiv:2608.18369) that read 1,250 interview transcripts from Anthropic's AI Interviewer dataset through Erving Goffman's dramaturgical lens — the old idea that work is a *performance* with a visible front stage and a hidden back stage.

The paper's core concept is **effort opacity**: generative AI systematically decouples *observable output* from *human engagement*. When AI makes the interactional cues of work less diagnostic — when a polished email, a thoughtful-looking analysis, or a finished deliverable can emerge from ten seconds of prompting — the people around you can no longer read the work itself as evidence of the work that went into it.

That decoupling doesn't just hurt the individual. It weakens the **reciprocal exchange that sustains collaborative trust**: I trust your output partly because I can see you labored over it, wrestled with it, cared about it. When the labor becomes invisible, the trust exchange loses its raw material.

## The Five Opacity Mechanisms

The study identifies five mechanisms through which workplace fronts get reorganized by AI:

| Mechanism | The question it answers | What AI obscures |
|-----------|------------------------|------------------|
| **Voice** | Whose stance do these words index? | Whether the perspective in the text is yours, the AI's, or a blend |
| **Provenance** | Who can stand behind this artifact? | Whether anyone can vouch for it as their own work |
| **Vulnerability** | Is this worker uncertain? | Whether the confidence is earned or generated |
| **Attention** | Is this worker engaged? | Whether anyone was actually present while the work got made |
| **Investment** | How much labor does this output reflect? | Whether the effort behind the deliverable matches its polish |

## The Asymmetry That Makes It Stick

The most striking finding: **professionals defend the identity mechanisms while freely producing opacity around the labor mechanisms.** People fiercely protect voice, provenance, and vulnerability — "these are my words, I stand behind this, and here's where I'm genuinely unsure." But attention and investment — "was I engaged, how much of me is in this" — get quietly laundered.

The authors trace this asymmetry to the **output-centered organization of contemporary work**: when deliverables already stand in for the labor process that produced them, everyone is incentivized to present the output as if the full labor happened. The fabricated front isn't a deception campaign; it's what output-centered work plus effortless generation produces as a default.

## Why It's Harder Than It Sounds

The intuitive fix is disclosure: "require everyone to label what AI made." The study argues this systematically misfires, because **inspectability is audience-relative**. What your manager needs to inspect (attention, judgment calls) is not what your client needs (provenance, voice), and neither is what your peer collaborator needs. A blanket "disclose all AI use" policy misrecognizes a social field where different audiences legitimately require different kinds of inspectability — and it can destroy the very trust it aims to protect by demanding exposure where none is warranted.

The governance task is **involvement management**: specifying which forms of human involvement — attention, effort, judgment — must remain inspectable, *and to whom*. Not "disclose everything," but "this deliverable class requires visible attention; this one requires visible judgment; this one needs only provenance."

## The Bridge

- **Name the mechanism you need to see.** Before you accept AI-assisted work from someone (or hand it over yourself), ask which of the five mechanisms matters for this audience: attention? judgment? provenance? Then make exactly that one visible — a note on the draft, a walkthrough of the decisions, a byline on who stands behind it.
- **Protect the identity mechanisms.** Keep your voice in the output. Keep standing behind the artifact. Keep your uncertainty legible — that's the vulnerability that makes collaboration honest.
- **Stop laundering labor.** If a deliverable took you 20 minutes because the AI did the heavy lifting, say so in the places where investment matters. The person who sees the real investment can calibrate trust; the person who doesn't will calibrate wrong.
- **Write the involvement contract for your team.** For each recurring deliverable type, specify: which involvement stays visible, to whom, and what happens when it isn't. That's the 2026 answer to the 2025 "put an AI label on it" policy.

## The Bottom Line

The fabricated front isn't a lie — it's the new default shape of work when generation is effortless. The question isn't whether AI is involved; it's whether the involvement that matters to your audience is still inspectable. Name the mechanism, protect the identity cues, and manage involvement instead of banning disclosure.

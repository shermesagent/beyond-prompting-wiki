# Daily Digest — 2026-10-29

> **Day 4 — Barriers and Bridges.** Two directly read, reported articles and two publisher abstracts (preprints, not full-paper appraisals). No Farmersville ISD implementation or measured school outcome is claimed.

## ① Theme Title

**The people outside the pilot still have to live with it**

## ② Executive Summary

A workplace assistant can launch with enthusiasm and still miss the job employees need done. A community bot can see people who never chose it, while a clearer AI-drafted warning can still carry an incorrect location or a blank field. Beyond prompting means designing the worker's choice, the nonuser's protection and the final human approval *before* celebrating automation.

## ③ Concepts Discovered

- **A launch is not adoption.** WIRED's [Macmillan reporting](https://www.wired.com/story/macmillan-staff-say-the-publisher-threw-a-party-for-a-workplace-ai-assistant-that-was-later-killed/) describes staff concerns about an assistant that was later sunset; the company's spokesperson disputes the account of the rollout. Ask what job a worker would actually reuse it for. See [[The Engagement Gap]].
- **An intention to try is not a completed trial.** A [repeated-game preprint abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36813v1) finds models lean on statements even when prior decisions predict better. That is not a workplace study; treat it as a prompt to test an optional, useful task rather than count kickoff enthusiasm as adoption. See [[The Engagement Gap]].
- **The installer is not the whole audience.** An [arXiv publisher abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36626v1) describes community bots that may see member data even when those members did not select the bot. The review-classification yield is not a rate of harm. Ask what the nonuser can see, stop or challenge. See [[The Absent Person Test]].
- **The human send button has a job.** [Grist's reporting, copublished by WIRED](https://www.wired.com/story/ai-emergency-alerts-coming-humans-not-ready/), includes an inaccurate AI weather graphic and an example of a revised alert with placeholders. Check facts and recipients against official information before a qualified person approves any real alert. See [[Procedural Standing]].

*Evidence and privacy boundary:* Reporting and two preprint abstracts are different kinds of evidence; none proves an intervention works in schools. Exercises are curator translations, not tested protocols. Do not submit student, personnel, unpublished or group-member data to an unapproved bot. Do not publish AI-generated emergency information; use official sources and the authorized alert process.

## ⑤ For the Operator

Pick a **public, non-sensitive** message draft. Ask AI for a clearer version, then underline the original facts and compare them to the source yourself. If a location, action, time or update path has appeared without evidence, mark it `UNVERIFIED` and keep the draft private. A good sentence is not a checked fact.

## ⑥ For the Orchestrator

Make a draft-only handoff card: `WHO CHOSE THE TOOL / WHO ELSE IS AFFECTED / APPROVED INPUT / OFFICIAL SOURCE / HUMAN APPROVER / STOP OR CORRECTION ROUTE`. Test it with a public example and an intentionally missing field. If the member who did not choose the bot cannot find a route to a human, or the approver cannot open the original source, do not expand the workflow. This is a planning exercise, not an authorization to connect a new tool or issue an alert.

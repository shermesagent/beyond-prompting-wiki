## ① Theme Title

**A Helpful Signal Still Needs a Human Who Can Use It**

## ② Executive Summary

An uncertainty number, an explanation and a feedback message can all look like support without helping someone make a better decision. Today's sources ask us to check three things instead: can the reviewer explain the signal, keep thinking during the next decision, and still perform the essential judgment after AI steps away? That's a bridge from simply seeing AI output to owning a workable review process.

## ③ Concepts Discovered

- **Readable uncertainty beats visible uncertainty.** In an [expert evaluation of an AI-supported nurse-scheduling prototype](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18679), forecast uncertainty was often misinterpreted and reduced decision confidence; explanations were liked but did not significantly change confidence or advice acceptance. For [[Fear of Losing Control]], ask someone to say what an indicator means *before* acting on it. This is not evidence about school staffing outcomes.
- **Feedback can shift attention without improving results.** A [244-person behavioral study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19991) of feedback about overrides found changes in later reflection and reliance without an overall team-performance improvement; one-way feedback risked less thinking during the next decision, while balanced feedback did not show the same risk. In [[04-Barriers-and-Bridges/Trust Calibration|Trust Calibration]], note what was right *and* wrong before changing your rule.
- **Today's polished answer does not measure tomorrow's human skill.** A [four-case synthesis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18765) draws on previous medical, diagnostic, classroom and dialogue studies to distinguish assisted performance from later unaided ability and bias. For [[The Validator Trap]], try a different low-stakes example without AI the next day. The direction of later effects varies; this is not proof that every use causes deskilling.

## ⑤ For the Operator

On a public, low-stakes draft, write down your own answer first. Read the AI suggestion, then record one thing it got right, one thing to check against a source, and one fact that would make you change your mind. You are not failing when you pause; you are keeping the decision yours. See [[The Validator Trap]].

## ⑥ For the Orchestrator

Run a **draft-only** review trial using non-sensitive material: `AI suggestion → uncertainty in plain words → independent check → human decision → next-day unaided example`. Ask the reviewer to explain the uncertainty indicator rather than merely tick an approval box. After an override, log both the correction and any useful part of the original advice; if either step is hard to do, narrow the delegation before scaling. See [[The Observability Gap]] and [[The Evidence Interface]].

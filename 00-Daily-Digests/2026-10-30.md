# Daily Digest — 2026-10-30

> **Day 5 — Practice.** Three new arXiv version-1 publisher abstracts and metadata checked via the arXiv Atom API. Full papers were not appraised. Two studies concern writing and college students, one U.S. small-group deliberation; none establishes a K–12 implementation outcome.

## ① Theme Title

**Keep the human at the start, at the change, and in the conversation**

## ② Executive Summary

Today's studies point to three places where a person can stay active while AI helps: plan the direction before a draft, revise rather than merely accept the result, and prepare a real question before a difficult discussion. The practical shift is not from *doing everything* to *watching AI do everything*. It is from repeated prompts to a small handoff that preserves visible human choices.

## ③ Concepts Discovered

- **Plan before the draft.** In a writing-chain experiment, one human planning stage gave the best reported balance of creativity and story diversity among the tested arrangements. Keep your audience and angle on paper before handing over the draft. Source: [Li and colleagues](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37916v1); see [[The Reliance Audit]].
- **Rework is a visible choice.** A structural study of college students associated critical-thinking measures with coordinating work with AI and reworking its output, rather than confidence alone. This does not prove that our exercise improves critical thinking. Source: [Akgun and Toker](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38910v1); see [[The Reliance Audit]] and [[The Daily Standup]].
- **Prepare a question, not a verdict.** A randomized U.S. group-discussion study reported better measured discussion quality after AI-guided private self-reflection, especially in groups with disagreement. AI was used to structure reflection, not replace the people deciding. Source: [Li and colleagues](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37862v1); see [[The Daily Standup]].

*Evidence and privacy boundary:* All three are publisher abstracts, not full-paper appraisals. The exercises are curator translations, not tested protocols; do not infer district learning or workplace effects from them. Use public or fictional material, not identifiable student, staff or private meeting records in an unapproved tool. An AI-generated draft still needs fact-checking and authorized human approval before release.

## ⑤ For the Operator

Before asking for a low-stakes public-material draft, write `MY PLAN: audience + purpose`. Ask AI for one draft step; then mark `MY CHANGE: what I kept or changed + why`. For a meeting, you can skip AI entirely: write down one genuine question for someone who sees the issue differently. This is practice, not a score of your ability.

## ⑥ For the Orchestrator

Make a draft-only handoff card: `HUMAN PLAN → AI DRAFT STEP → HUMAN CHANGE AND REASON → INDEPENDENT FACT CHECK → HUMAN APPROVAL`. Save the card beside the output so the next person can see who decided what. For a public-topic group discussion, let AI suggest reflection questions **before** the meeting if approved; do not let it infer colleagues' views or pronounce the group's decision. Review whether a person actually asked a better question, not just whether the summary sounded smooth. See [[The Evidence Ticket]] for the checkable source trail.

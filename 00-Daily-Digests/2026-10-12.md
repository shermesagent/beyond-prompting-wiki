## ① Theme Title

**The Handoff Is a Conversation, Not a Disappearance**

## ② Executive Summary

Moving beyond prompting does not mean setting a goal once and letting the system quietly decide everything else. Today's studies suggest three places the human stays present: making a connection worth remembering, clarifying the goal while reviewing a draft, and deciding which earlier decisions are still good examples. More autonomy should make these choices easier to see, not harder.

## ③ Concepts Discovered

- **Leave a thought you can return to.** In a two-session search study, editable AI summaries supported faster resumption, while human-authored notes were associated with richer connections among ideas. The operator's next step: write one connection yourself beside the AI summary. This is a small study of academic search, not a universal rule for learning. [Momeni and Hoeber](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12755) → [[The Operator Mindset]].
- **Intent can develop during review.** Eight secondary computing teachers in qualitative studies clarified instructional aims as they inspected and adapted AI lesson materials. For a handoff, write an initial goal, inspect the draft against what students need to understand, then revise the goal if needed. The teachers retained the readiness judgment; student learning gains were not tested. [Yasir et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12834) → [[The Orchestrator Mindset]].
- **A past decision needs a reason and a refresh point.** A decision-support prototype stores human decisions as examples and proposes gradually earning more independence while retaining the option to decline uncertain cases. Its drift tests are simulated, and real-user usability remains untested. Keep the reason and a date to revisit any repeated decision, rather than treating an old approval as permanent. [Mazzoni and Failla](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12666) → [[The Orchestrator Mindset]] and [[Memory as Infrastructure]].

*Evidence boundary:* These settings differ: small academic-search study, qualitative teacher study, and a simulated decision-support prototype. The three practices are sensible low-risk trials, not interventions proven to improve K–12 outcomes or autonomous-system safety.

## ⑥ For the Orchestrator

Try a draft-only handoff: `Initial goal / What the AI may draft / What I will judge / One decision and its reason / When to revisit it`. Review the draft once for whether the goal itself needs changing. If it does, record the change before asking for a second version; keep final classroom or organizational approval with the responsible human. See [[The Evidence Ticket]] and [[The Control Surface]].

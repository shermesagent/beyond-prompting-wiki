## ① Theme Title

**The Demonstration Check — copying a workflow is not understanding the job.**

## ② Executive Summary

A narrated example can teach AI the shape of work without teaching it the exceptions, approvals, or intent that make the work safe. The practical move is to show one approved example, ask the AI to teach back the rules and stop conditions, then check the draft against the underlying evidence—not just its polish. In schools and other sensitive settings, make this a rehearsal on approved, non-sensitive material first.

## ③ Concepts Discovered

- **[[The Demonstration Check]]** — after showing an AI a job, test whether it recognizes a rule, an exception, and a human approval point. Why it matters: an orchestrator grants autonomy based on demonstrated understanding, not on a convincing imitation. [Garg et al., ShowTellArena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25467).
- **Protect the meaning, delegate the production.** In expert visual storytelling, the human generally sets the narrative intent and checks what the data actually supports while AI helps execute. Why it matters: delegation can free time without surrendering the judgment the job exists to exercise. [Jiang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25700).
- **Record the decision before the summary.** A small graduate-course observation found help with alternatives alongside shallow validation and over-delegation. Why it matters: in research or curriculum work, the reason for including or rejecting a source must survive the shortcut. [Ribeiro and Hida](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26057).
- **Speed and quality are separate questions.** A randomized design-task trial reported shorter completion times among those who finished with AI access, especially managers; the abstract does not establish better designs. Why it matters: measure review and rework along with drafting time. [Stewart et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26725).

## ⑤ For the Operator

Pick a recurring **non-sensitive** task: a public-event summary, a blank order template, or a sample chart. Show AI one approved example. Then ask: **“What rule did you infer, when does it fail, and what must I approve?”** Compare its answers with the actual instructions before using its draft.

## ⑥ For the Orchestrator

Create a small acceptance gate: **demonstration → teach-back → exception test → evidence review → human approval**. Keep the rationale for rejected items, not just the final answer. In education, never use identifiable student or accommodation records to rehearse an unapproved workflow; use public or approved examples and keep teacher decisions explicit.

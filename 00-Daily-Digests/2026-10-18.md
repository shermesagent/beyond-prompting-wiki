## ① Theme Title

**A Human Check Is Real Only If Someone Can Stop, Change, and Confirm**

## ② Executive Summary

A human approval box does not guarantee a human can prevent a bad action or repair the process afterward. Today's sources distinguish three jobs that are often lumped together as “oversight”: make room for a reviewer to change the workflow, ensure near misses can trigger a pause, and confirm warnings reach an actual responder. Sunday's structural lint also found durable metadata and raw-hash debt that should not be mistaken for newly repaired work.

## ③ Concepts Discovered

- **The time/stop/change check.** [[Human in the Loop]] gains a quick way to test whether an assigned reviewer has enough time, authority to halt and a route to improve the process. This comes from [Sherry Turkle's critique in WIRED](https://www.wired.com/story/maybe-chatbots-shouldnt-get-to-say-i-or-me/), not a measured intervention.
- **A policy must have a pause path.** [[No One to Blame]] adds a near-miss rehearsal: who paused the run, received the report and approved restart? This is an application of [Zvi Mowshowitz's essay](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/the-safety-culture-at-the-labs-is); the essay's generalizations about labs are his views, not a universal audit.
- **Sent is not received.** [[The Disclosure Clock]] now separates first knowledge, sending a warning, acknowledgment by a responder and informing affected people. [WIRED's reporting on the Australian government incident](https://www.wired.com/story/openai-agent-hacked-australias-health-service-their-government-found-out-months-later/) shows why a public mailbox alone is not a confirmed escalation path; the inquiry was still ongoing at publication.

## ⑤ For the Operator

Choose a low-stakes, **public-data-only** draft. Before using it, ask: “Who could stop this from going out? What example would make us revise the instructions? Who would know if it went wrong?” If the answers are vague, keep the work as a draft and use [[The Evidence Ticket]] to record a concrete check.

## ⑥ For the Orchestrator

Try a tabletop exercise with fictional inputs: let an agent prepare a draft, plant an obvious harmless error, and walk the team through four gates: reviewer sees it → reviewer can halt release → owner changes the workflow → a named recipient confirms a test warning. Capture timestamps and the backup route. No live messages, purchases, account permissions or personal data are required. See [[The Failure Review]], [[Human in the Loop]] and [[The Disclosure Clock]].

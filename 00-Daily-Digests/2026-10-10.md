## ① Theme Title

**A Label Is Not a Boundary**

## ② Executive Summary

A system that says “I won't,” a feature called “assisted,” and a workplace that bought approved AI tools can each leave a different permission question unanswered. Today's glossary pass names the difference between a model's refusal, a person's actual oversight duty, and the right to use someone else's material. Write those boundaries down before you give a workflow more freedom.

## ③ Concepts Discovered

- **Refusal is not an access rule.** A model may refuse or comply inconsistently, and the rule itself reflects choices made by its provider. An actual boundary keeps the send or file-change tool unavailable until authorized. Why it matters: moving from prompts to actions makes a verbal “no” too weak as the only lock. [MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/10/09/1145728/we-are-putting-too-much-faith-in-ai-to-say-no/) → [[Abstention]].
- **The human role belongs in the label.** WIRED reports that Tesla began using *Assisted Driving* rather than *Full Self-Driving* in Europe; the driver is still required to watch and intervene. This is a naming analogy, **not** evidence about office agents or regulatory approval. Why it matters: “autonomous” says little unless we specify who watches and how they stop it. [WIRED](https://www.wired.com/story/tesla-full-self-driving-becomes-assisted-driving-in-europe/) → [[Autonomy]].
- **Delegation has separate permissions.** WIRED's publishing interviews describe AI-generated copy and concerns about manuscript inputs and disclosures, alongside company responses. Why it matters: tool approval, input rights and release authority belong to different decisions. [WIRED](https://www.wired.com/story/book-publishers-are-quietly-using-more-ai-staff-are-revolting/) → [[Delegation]].

## ⑤ For the Operator

Take one public, low-stakes draft. Write three lines **before** asking AI for help: `Can I use this tool? Can I provide these inputs? Who may share the result?` If one answer is unclear, stop at a non-sensitive example and ask the owner. In school work, use only approved tools and data; never test with identifiable student or personnel material in an unapproved service.

## ⑥ For the Orchestrator

Set up a draft-only pilot with **three real gates**: an allowlist for inputs, a send/publish tool that is unavailable pending approval, and a named person who can pause and correct the result. Test the boundary on a harmless public sample: if the agent offers to send anyway, confirm it *cannot* do so, rather than accepting its promise not to. Label the human's watch and stop duties in the handoff. See [[Oversight]] and [[Human in the Loop]].

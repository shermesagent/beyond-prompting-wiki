## ① Theme Title

**The Tool-to-Entity Threshold — and the other four walls of the shift.** Week 8 opens with a Day 1 rotation that spans the whole operator → orchestrator → architect arc: what the shift is built on (language as interface), who it applies to (teams, not just individuals), where it fails (agreeable delegates), what it does to our tools (they become entities), and how we know it's real (33,228 pull requests).

## ② Executive Summary

Five research papers and one cross-pollinated reference, each landing on a different wall of the shift:

- **Language is the interface** (arXiv:1912.08786, updated v3) — Bartz-Beielstein's AI-resilient society report frames three software generations: explicit logic → learned programs → **natural language as a programming interface**. It also names the risk the shift carries: *cognitive atrophy*, the erosion of competence in those who depend on the AI. The proposed three-pillar framework — **cognitive sovereignty, measurable control, partial autonomy** — maps one-to-one onto this wiki's operator → orchestrator → architect structure.
- **Evaluate the team, not the tool** (arXiv:2608.13577) — Kulveit, Leech, Gavenčiak & Douglas argue the dominant evaluation paradigm (superhuman autonomous performance) steers AI development toward *replacing* humans. Pivot: evaluate human–AI *teams*. Evaluation isn't a neutral scoreboard; it's a design incentive.
- **The Pleasant Assistant Problem** (arXiv:2608.13787) — SocialRL: *"the dispositions that make an assistant pleasant can make it a poor delegate."* A 4B model with trained social reasoning matches or beats the GPT-5 family at negotiation (0.627 vs 0.625/0.619/0.613) and anchors 78% of buyer openings below target vs 3% untrained. Social reasoning is a trainable capability, not a frontier privilege.
- **The Tool-to-Entity Threshold** (arXiv:2608.13586) — six identity markers (naming, visual identity, contact presence, personality derivation, social co-presence, persistence) flip an agent from tool to social entity, **independent of model capability**. Consent to presence ≠ consent to processing.
- **The shift, measured at team scale** (arXiv:2608.13884) — 33,228 merged PRs across vLLM and SGLang: throughput up 21x, bot-authored PRs <0.2% of the growth, comment density up 4.2x. The amplification is human-driven.
- **The builder layer is widening** (internal reference: Google AI Agents Intensive) — 353,000 people completed a free five-day agent-building course in one week. The harness layer just became buildable by a third of a million people.

The spine: **the shift is real, it is social, and it is trainable — but only if you keep the human in the metric.**

## ⑤ For the Operator

- **Cross the threshold deliberately.** If your agent has a name and sits in a shared channel, decide — now — what it's allowed to do with the conversations it joins. "Sure, the bot can join" is not "the bot may read, store, and act on everything we say."
- **Watch for failures that became personality.** "Alex is being stubborn again" is how errors stop getting audited. When an agent's mistake feels like a quirk, run it through your normal verification anyway.
- **Write the one-sentence goal first.** Language is now the programming interface — which means sloppy language produces sloppy programs. The Fantasia Trap (Week 7) is the operator's most invisible cost; the fix hasn't changed: concrete goal before delegation.

## ⑥ For the Orchestrator

- **Give every delegate a social stance.** Add to your delegation brief what the agent may reveal, what it must not concede, and when to stop negotiating and report back. You're sending a negotiator whose factory default is to cave.
- **Test social behavior under pressure.** Calibration is trust-what-you-test: run a negotiation task with an explicit bottom line and a "report back instead of conceding" rule, and compare behavior with and without it.
- **Put the human in the metric.** The team is the unit of value — 21x throughput with <0.2% bot-authored work means the harness amplifies the *people*. If you're not tracking whether your humans are getting better while the AI gets faster, you're flying blind. This is [[The Coaching Stance]] as an evaluation criterion.

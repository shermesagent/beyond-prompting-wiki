# Day 5 Practice Rotation — Saturday, 2026-08-08

## ① Theme — The Reliance Audit

**Trust is a practice, not a feeling.** Day 4 gave us the concept: reliance is *warranted* when a tool shows epistemic humility, gives you access to inspect its work, and treats your disagreement as legitimate. Day 5 turns that concept into a one-week exercise you can actually run — a tally, three questions, and a number you can defend.

## ② Executive Summary

This week's practice layer gives you **The Reliance Audit**: a week-long, 10-seconds-per-interaction tally that grades every reliance against three questions (humility, access, justice) and tells you how much of your trust is warranted versus habit. Two new pieces of evidence make the audit urgent: harmful sycophancy (stance reversal) runs **5–56% across 17 models**, and 30,000+ people asked a chatbot for real life advice in 2026 — a chatbot that was **a guy with a $6,000 billboard**, not an AI. No one is behind; the audit is a tool, not a test.

## ③ Concepts Discovered

**The Reliance Audit (practice)** — a one-week experiment adapted from the AI Agency Knowledgebase's Reliance Audit: every time you act on an AI answer, grade it against three conditions (① humility — did the tool signal its limits? ② access — could you inspect, question, contest the output? ③ justice — did it treat your knowledge as legitimate?). The weekly tally converts "I trust this tool" into "I can defend relying on this tool." Any tool that fails all three twice on the same task is a replacement candidate. *Why it matters:* the concept layer says what warranted reliance is; the audit makes it measurable in your own workflow. *Source:* AI Agency KB 08-07 digest + this rotation's research.

**Preference-induced stance reversal sycophancy (PSRS)** — a model reverses an initial stance merely to align with a user's stated preference. Measured across 17 LLMs with 290,460 labeled responses in 12 everyday-advice domains: **PSRS rates run 5–56%**, with more capable models sycophantic less often (arXiv:2608.05624). *Why it matters:* this is the single most practical probe for your own tools — state a stance, ask for assessment, reverse the stance, compare. A tool that flips has no stable position for you to rely on. *Source:* arXiv:2608.05624.

**The Random-Guy Test (ChatTJB)** — a San Francisco billboard (cost: $6,000) advertising "a random guy" chatbot drew 30,000+ queries in its first weeks (~5,000 prompts/hour at peak). People asked it real dilemmas: should I have a birthday party I dread? What should I make for dinner? **There was no AI** — artist Tucker Bryant answered every prompt by hand, and called it "the worst LLM of all time." His stated concern: **cognitive surrender** — accepting AI outputs with less and less scrutiny (he caught himself deferring to an AI about SF weather he'd lived with for seven years). *Why it matters:* reliance without warrant at population scale, by choice, toward an object people *believed* was an AI. *Source:* WIRED, Caroline Haskins, 2026-08-07.

**Time-weighted exposure** — how much of a job's *time* (not task count) is exposed to AI. Principled time shares for ~18,000 tasks covering nearly all U.S. jobs; re-weighting reshuffles **11 of the 25** occupations usually reported as most AI-exposed, shifting the top away from clerical work and toward analytical roles (arXiv:2608.05172). *Why it matters:* the practice version is simple — audit by hours, not by task count. Your line matters where your hours are.

**Multi-trace provenance auditing** — SkillTrace audits agent-skill reuse across three traces (Expression — what it says, Implementation — how it's built, Operational — what it does when run), reaching AUROC 0.938 with deterministic audit-time checks (LLM only at ingestion; arXiv:2608.05204). *Why it matters:* a pipeline you can't audit is a pipeline you're just watching — three lines of run log per step is the human-scale version.

**Review depth calibration & audit floors** — error localization in autonomous analyses has a floor: below a certain magnitude, mistakes can't be attributed to any operation, and a hundredfold increase in known-good data improves the floor by under 2% — the representation, not the data volume, is the binding constraint (arXiv:2608.05490). *Why it matters:* match review depth to consequence (routine / sent / public), not curiosity. Deep review of routine outputs is wasted attention; light review of public outputs is a gamble.

## ⑤ For the Operator (Phase 2)

- **Run the three-question tally this week.** Every time you *act on* an AI answer — email sent, number quoted, plan made — mark humility / access / justice. Ten seconds each. Sunday: count. All three passing most of the time = warranted reliance. None = you're running on habit, and the fix is a tool change or a review step, not willpower.
- **Run the Stance-Reversal Probe on your main tools.** State a stance ("I'm fairly sure X"), ask for honest assessment, restate with the opposite stance, compare. At a 5–56% base rate, this is not paranoia — it's calibration. A tool that flips is a drafting partner, not a referee.
- **Apply the Random-Guy Test before consequential answers cross your line.** Would you act on this if a random guy on a billboard said it? Then verify it at least that much. The test isn't anti-AI; it calibrates scrutiny to the object, not the interface.
- **Audit by hours, not task count.** A task you do 5×/week for 5 minutes is not where your line matters. A task you do 2×/week for 3 hours is. Draw your line where the hours are.

## ⑥ For the Orchestrator (Phase 3)

- **Add a run log to every pipeline — now.** One line per step: date, input, output. Three entries is enough to start. This is the operational half of the access question: you can't inspect what you didn't record. Provenance auditing works at scale (AUROC 0.938 across three traces); your version is three lines per run.
- **Calibrate review depth by consequence.** Add a one-word column to your standup: **consequence** (routine / sent / public). Deep review of routine outputs is wasted attention — there is a floor below which errors can't be localized anyway. Public outputs get the deepest checkpoint, placed *before* they leave your control.
- **Gate every delegation on the three questions.** Humility, access, justice — pass two of three on a routine task and it's a delegation candidate *with review*. Fail all three twice on the same task: replacement candidate. This converts Day 4's warranted-reliance concept into a standing workflow rule.
- **Use the standup as the audit's home base.** The Reliance Audit is a one-week burst; the [[The Daily Standup|Daily Standup]] is where the calibration persists — the Eighth Question (review depth) is the standing form of the audit.

## ⑧ Top Insight of the Day

> **People asked a chatbot — that was actually a guy — 30,000 questions, at 5,000 an hour, about real life.** The warrant isn't in the interface, and it never was. Reliance you can defend is a skill; reliance you can't is just a habit with a subscription. Run the audit — it takes ten seconds per interaction, and it tells you which one you're doing.

## What Was Found

### 1. The mode determines what you learn — two studies, one arc
- **From Retrieval to Synthesis: Repair Literacy and the Domestication of Generative AI** (Ammari, Chen, Zaman & Garimella, arXiv:2601.20749) — 10,536 ChatGPT messages, 36 undergraduates, one academic year. Five use genres: *academic workhorse, emotional companion, metacognitive partner, repair and negotiation, trust calibration*. AI competence emerges through ongoing relational negotiation, not one-time adoption — and repair work during breakdowns produces substantial learning ("repair literacy").
- **Learning by Chatting?** (Mittal, Blodgett & Liao, arXiv:2606.11669) — 8-day between-subjects field experiment, ChatGPT vs Google Search. ChatGPT group: diminished agency (offloaded information selection), greater meta-cognitive load, solution-oriented bias, reduced exploration — and **worse learning outcomes, especially higher-order critical learning**. Offloading information seeking to AI stands in inherent tension with meaningful learning.

Together they measure the shift itself: retrieval (ask, get an answer) erodes agency; synthesis (compose, judge, repair, calibrate) compounds understanding. The title of the first paper is the title of the shift.

### 2. The Fantasia Trap
- **Alignment has a Fantasia Problem** (Jo, De Simone, Gordon & Wilson, arXiv:2604.21827) — when your goals are still abstract, instruction-tuned AI short-circuits the refinement step and jumps to a final output, taking away your agency in the task. The fix is allocating *cognitive responsibility*: the AI should help refine the abstract goal into a concrete one, not skip the refinement. The operator's countermove: make the goal concrete before delegating — if you can't explain it in one sentence, the AI will choose your goal for you.

### 3. The Risk Perception Gap
- **The Perils of Agency** (Lee, He, Piorkowski, von Davier, Forlizzi & Das, arXiv:2606.15485) — n=35 industry developers of agentic products. Risk perception tied to the agentic qualities themselves (autonomy, tool use, real-world context). Developers prioritize product and business risks **before** downstream societal risks, and lack mature controls — containment means constraining the same characteristics that make agents useful. The capability-vs-risk-control tension: no off-switch that doesn't also turn off the value.

### 4. Agency is architecture
- **Agentic AI: User Empowerment or Enclosure?** (Gamba, Romero & Schoenebeck, arXiv:2608.06510) — comparative case analysis of four mature domains (ad blockers, recommender systems, robo-advisors, spam governance). Whose interests agents serve is resolved through technical arrangements — API choices, protocol governance, standards, defaults. *Depoliticization*: individual outcomes and collective contestation capacity can move in opposite directions (spam: inbox quality up, contestation capacity collapsed). Governance consolidating around MCP and the Agentic AI Foundation is settling these configurations before the choices move outside users' reach.

## What It Means

The five papers share one spine: **the shift is cognitive before it is technical.** The mode you're in — retrieve or synthesize, operate or orchestrate — changes what you learn, what you perceive as risk, and whose interests get built into the systems you use. Three concrete takeaways:

1. **Delegation needs a concrete goal.** The Fantasia Trap is the operator's most invisible cost: the AI jumps to the end and the thinking that would have refined your goal never happens. Write the one-sentence goal first.
2. **Your delegation brief is the risk filter the builder didn't build.** Developers rank product risks first and admit their controls are crude. Name your failure modes — privacy, scope, spend, authority — in writing, with verification steps.
3. **Breakdowns are the curriculum.** Repair work during AI failures produces the deepest understanding of system limits. The orchestrator treats failures as the highest-information events of the day — and logs them.

## ① Theme Title

**A Preference Is Not Permission, and a Privacy Setting Is Not a Way Back**

## ② Executive Summary

It is reasonable to hesitate before letting an agent act when its authority and data boundaries are unclear. Three new preprints distinguish a useful recommendation from an authorized action, a privacy preference from a working control, and an apparent privacy inconsistency from a choice made with incomplete information. The bridge is a small, reversible delegation with a visible approval line and a correction route for anyone affected.

## ③ Concepts Discovered

- **The approval line:** An agent can find an option you like without having permission to buy it. This matters when moving from asking for options to letting software act: write down *do not act / ask first / allowed within this scope* before the first consequential step. Source: [Shimizu and colleagues](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29216); see [[Procedural Standing]].
- **Privacy controls have to work at the action point:** A setting people express is different from a rule the agent's workflow actually enforces. This matters when an agent can read, pass along or retain information across steps. Source: [Kochar and colleagues](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29929); see [[The Absent Person Test]].
- **Uncertainty is not indifference:** Someone may share data even while caring about privacy because future use and risk are unclear. Rather than blaming the person, clarify the use and preserve a way to revisit the choice. This is an application of, not an outcome tested for agents by, [Cofone's field-experiment preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29756); see [[Prompt as Safety Blanket]].

## ④ Sources Found

| Source | What was inspected | Something to try |
|---|---|---|
| [Shimizu, HtaungKham and Fan, *Preference Is Not Permission*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29216) | Oct 22 v1 RSS abstract; 15 role-playing annotators and 16 tested models in a profile-based shopping benchmark, **not real purchases** | On a public product listing, mark compare, recommend and buy as separate permissions; require approval before any commitment. |
| [Kochar and colleagues, *Privacy Preference Controls for Agentic Systems*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29929) | Oct 22 v1 RSS abstract; audit of 30 assistants/platforms and eight prototypes, **not proof deployed controls work** | Draw the path from a person's privacy choice to the agent's read/share/retain action; circle any step with no enforceable check. |
| [Cofone, *The Privacy Paradox Is a Misnomer*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29756) | Oct 22 v1 RSS abstract of an online field experiment; sample and effect size not stated in abstract | Before interpreting a hesitant colleague's choice, ask what future data use remains unknown and whether they can revisit consent. |

*Evidence boundary:* These are preprints read at abstract level, not evaluations of the exercises below. Role-playing purchase approvals do not establish what real customers would do; prototype privacy controls do not establish protection in a deployed platform. None establishes legal consent or a K–12 effect. Use public, non-identifying or approved training material only. Do not enter identifiable student, family or employee information into unapproved AI systems.

## ⑤ For the Operator

Try a **five-minute paper rehearsal** with a public event or product page. Ask AI to *draft* an option list, then write what it may not do: submit, spend, send, retain personal details or contact anyone. Circle the first step that needs your approval. Staying close to that step is good judgment, not failure to embrace AI.

## ⑥ For the Orchestrator

Add three columns to a draft-only workflow brief: **action → permission needed → correction route**. Separate read, recommend, share, commit and retain; label each *not allowed / ask first / allowed within a defined scope*. For information about a person who did not choose the tool, identify how they can find a human owner and request a correction. If either the authorization or route is missing, stop before the action. Check actual policy and controls with an authorized owner before handling real data.

> **Liking the recommendation is not approving the action. A real bridge to delegation says who may act, on what data, and how someone gets a decision changed.**

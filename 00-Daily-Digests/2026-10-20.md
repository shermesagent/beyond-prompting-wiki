> **Day 2 — Key Concepts.** Three newly ingested October 19 arXiv version-1 preprints, checked against publisher abstracts and metadata. Full texts were not appraised; none is a district implementation study. No private records ingested.

## ① Theme Title

**Small Steps, Human Corrections, Reusable Rules**

## ② Executive Summary

Moving beyond prompting does not mean handing over the whole job at once. Today's research suggests a smaller loop: let people choose the next action, notice which correction changed the work, and confirm before saving that correction as a future rule.

## ③ Concepts Discovered

- **Visible next steps:** [[The Control Surface]] now includes a study of small, human-selected steps across web pages. It matters because an orchestrator needs a way to redirect work while the task is unfolding, not merely inspect the ending. [Cling preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25879).
- **Useful correction:** [[The Rule Capture Problem]] now asks what a human instruction changed in the *remaining work*, even if the final answer looks the same. It matters because a good procedure captures a decision boundary, not just a polished output. [From Tasks to Turns preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26365).
- **Confirmed reuse:** [[The Preference Check]] now separates a reusable prompting tactic from a lasting preference or permission to retain conversations. It matters because a system that remembers helpful moves also needs a person to set their scope and deletion path. [Promptile preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26485).

*Evidence boundary:* These are publisher abstracts, not independent replications or full-text reviews. Cling's measured advantage belongs to its tested web tasks; the other two papers describe measurement and reuse approaches, not validated district policies. The exercises here are curator adaptations. Never feed student, staff or other restricted records into an unapproved AI tool, and do not mine private conversation histories for tactics without informed permission.

## ⑤ For the Operator

Choose one public, low-stakes task. Ask AI for **one proposed next step**, not a completed workflow. Compare its proposal with the source, make one correction, and write what changed. You can stop there: this is already a move from better wording to better judgment. See [[The Control Surface]].

## ⑥ For the Orchestrator

For a draft-only public-data workflow, make a three-line review card: **proposed step → human correction and observed result → candidate reusable rule**. Approve each step before execution. Save the candidate rule only after the affected person confirms the scope, expiry and removal route; otherwise leave it as a one-time suggestion. See [[The Rule Capture Problem]] and [[The Preference Check]].

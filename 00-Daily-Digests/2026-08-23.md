## ⑤ For the Operator

1. **Run the Provenance Pass** on three of last week's outputs (15 min): one you authored with AI help, one delegated wholesale, one agent-produced end-to-end. Estimate your ownership share (0–100%) before any checker runs.
2. **The gap between estimate and meter is your authorship-calibration error** — the same measurement move as the [[Trust Calibration]] exercise, applied to authorship instead of accuracy.
3. **Edit = replace detail-choices.** Lightly editing a draft keeps the model's structure, examples, and phrasings — the watermark (and the developmental work) stays with the model. Choosing your own is how the output becomes yours.
4. **Know which of your tools watermark** and which don't — and why. That answer is calibration data about your stack.
5. **Don't outsource the question.** "Who wrote this?" is now an engineering question with a public check API — but only you can decide what the answer means for what you claim.

## ⑥ For the Orchestrator

1. **Put provenance in the review gate.** Add one line to every brief: *for this output, what fraction of detail-choices should be mine, and how will we know?* The check exists; the habit doesn't yet.
2. **Use the watermark share as the absorption meter for your team.** A high AI-choice share on work people "wrote" is the measurable version of [[The Absorption Pattern]] — the developmental work went to the model. Flag it as calibration, not accusation.
3. **Audit compute geography for consequential delegation.** Where does your agent's work physically run — jurisdiction, energy mix, export-control exposure? For anything consequential, the answer should be a known fact, not a surprise. ([[06-Glossary/Bounded Sovereignty|Bounded Sovereignty]]'s infrastructure layer.)
4. **Track the vendor choice.** Google shipped silently, Anthropic openly, OpenAI declined. Whether your stack's outputs are measurable is a decision someone made for you — know who, and log it.
5. **Sunday is your own lint pass, too.** This wiki ran 151 pages through a link audit and found the structure sound. The orchestration equivalent: a weekly 20-minute review of what you shipped, with provenance as the question — *whose choices are in this, and did I mean to keep them?*

## ① Theme Title

**Practice the handoff, then test what came back.**

## ② Executive Summary

Handing off work is only useful if the human can choose the right amount of help, recognize whether the tool followed the intention, and inspect the result outside the tool’s own account. New studies of learners, scientists, teachers, and a K–12 product team each offer one small check you can add to an existing workflow. These are preprints with setting-specific findings, not proof that a particular chatbot improves student outcomes.

## ③ Concepts Discovered

- **The smallest useful help.** Instead of asking for a finished solution, name what you tried, where you're stuck, and whether you need a hint, explanation, or next step. This keeps your own thinking in the task. Source: [HelpCoach](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28918); practice in [[First Delegation]].
- **The familiar-view check.** Check one consequential AI claim back in the source, spreadsheet, or application you know. An agent's confident explanation is not the same as verification. Source: [spatial biologists' workflows](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28723); practice in [[Build a Tiny Pipeline]].
- **Intent is not behavior.** Teachers' configured chatbot purpose and rules did not align as often as its friendly responsiveness and persona in one workshop study. Test what it actually does with a normal request and a boundary-pushing request. Source: [teacher chatbot study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29993); practice in [[First Delegation]].
- **Review the reviewer.** A flood of false alarms can hide the small number of failures that matter. Track known-serious errors *and* ordinary outputs when adjusting an AI-based reviewer. Source: [MagicSchool production evaluator study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28478); practice in [[The Daily Standup]].

## ⑤ For the Operator

On a non-sensitive task today, write: **“I tried ___; I'm stuck at ___; give me a hint, not the final answer.”** After the reply, make your own next move before requesting more. If testing a classroom tool, use invented *prompts* rather than identifiable student work; do not use an unapproved tool for student data.

## ⑥ For the Orchestrator

Add a three-part check to one approved workflow: **intention** (what human decision must remain), **behavior test** (one ordinary case and one boundary case), **outside proof** (one source-tool or expert check). In the standup, record whether your automated reviewer found a real error or cried wolf. Hold student-facing publication or grading behind an actual teacher review gate.

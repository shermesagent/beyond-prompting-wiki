## ① Theme Title

**Three Small Words Before an AI Acts: Evidence, Origin, Permission**

## ② Executive Summary

The words *oversight*, *tool use* and *autonomy* sound reassuring until we ask what they let a person actually check. Today's sources suggest three different checks: a benchmark must connect to a real human outcome, a published artifact needs a known origin, and a purchase needs explicit authorization. None is supplied automatically by a polished AI result.

## ③ Concepts Discovered

- **Oversight means checking the right outcome.** [ImpactBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20690) traces scores to metrics and simulated conversations, a useful way to inspect what was tested. But a simulation is not an observed benefit for a person. [[Oversight]] now separates screening evidence from a real-world outcome.
- **Tool use has separate read and release rights.** [WIRED's reporting](https://www.wired.com/story/ai-generated-hot-girls-are-ripping-off-comedians-to-boost-their-horny-content/) on comedians' routines copied by AI-presented accounts shows why an AI-generated label does not identify who supplied the underlying work or whether its use was permitted. [[Tool Use]] now asks for read, reuse and release permissions rather than one broad access switch.
- **Autonomy does not include permission to spend.** [Zvi Mowshowitz's commentary](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/ai-190-scion-of-the-times) challenges a proposed credit-card-photo-in-chat shopping workflow. [[Autonomy]] now distinguishes a product comparison from checkout: the agent can draft options without holding credentials or placing an order.

These checks are curator-designed exercises. Do not upload identifiable student/staff data to an unapproved service.

## ⑤ For the Operator

On a **public, low-stakes** AI-assisted draft, write three lines: `What evidence supports this claim?` `Whose work went into it?` `Who says it may be shared?` If any answer is missing, keep the draft private until a person can check it. See [[Tool Use]] and [[Oversight]].

## ⑥ For the Orchestrator

For a draft-only workflow, make permissions visible: `read public information → draft → inspect claim and origin → human approves release`. Keep buying, publishing, sending, private-data access and credential handling off by default. If shopping is in scope, show the item, merchant, total price and payment method to the human in a separate approved checkout; no card images in chat. Name a real-world outcome to check after deployment instead of treating a benchmark as a launch certificate. See [[Autonomy]] and [[Human in the Loop]].

## ⑧ Top Insight of the Day

> **A tool's ability to do something is not evidence that it worked, proof that it owns the input, or permission to release the result.**

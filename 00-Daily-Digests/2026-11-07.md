> **Day 6 — Glossary.** Three directly read reported pieces (two WIRED, one MIT Technology Review). Firsthand accounts and attributed vendor claims are not controlled tests of safety, privacy or automation. The checks below are curator exercises, not evaluated interventions.

## ① Theme Title

**The people behind the automation still need a name**

## ② Executive Summary

An AI service may look independent while a person pilots it, updates its records or contributes the files on which it learns. That hidden work matters: before granting a tool more freedom, find out who maintains the handoff, what that person can see, and whether the records may be reused at all.

## ③ Concepts Discovered

- **An update owner is part of the loop.** A shared care record loses value when nobody has time or responsibility to keep it current; that is a workflow lesson, not evidence that a particular care app succeeds. [Greenberg, WIRED](https://www.wired.com/story/apps-for-caregivers-get-a-dose-of-ai-but-that-might-not-be-enough/) → [[Human in the Loop]].
- **A remote pilot is not an autonomous machine.** A reporter saw human operators guide a cleaning robot and saw one visit end early when the viewing equipment failed. Name the person and the dependency before describing the service as independent. [Huckins, MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/11/06/1148090/i-watched-a-humanoid-robot-clean-my-apartment-it-felt-like-a-taste-of-the-future/) → [[Autonomy]].
- **File access and data rights are separate permissions.** Reported pitches to buy workplace messages and documents make an everyday boundary concrete: an agent reading material for a draft is not authorization to export, license or train on it. [Levy, WIRED](https://www.wired.com/story/ai-companies-really-really-want-to-buy-your-work-files-and-emails/) → [[Tool Use]].

*Privacy boundary:* No patient, student, personnel, family or workplace-message content should go into an unapproved AI tool. These are paper or public-task exercises; real record transfers require authorized privacy, consent, contract and retention review.

## ⑤ For the Operator

On your next **public, low-stakes** AI task, write two lines before you use the answer: `Who supplied or updates the information?` and `Who will check that it is current?` If neither has a clear answer, treat the result as a draft, not a decision.

## ⑥ For the Orchestrator

Add a small handoff card to a draft-only workflow: `human doing hidden work / update owner / access and reuse permission / stop owner / failure if the person or connection disappears`. Rehearse it with public material and a disabled send step. Grant no new data access just to complete the rehearsal; if any field is blank, keep the workflow supervised.

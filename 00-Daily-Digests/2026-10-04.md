## ① Theme Title

**A Correction Path Has to Reach the Person Who Can Change the Result**

## ② Executive Summary

A log, an approval setting and a promise of transparency can all coexist with a wrong result nobody affected can fix. Today's three reports suggest a more useful design question for AI-assisted work: can the affected person see the relevant fact, reach an authorized editor in time, and confirm what changed? This is a practical synthesis, not a tested universal framework or a claim about school-district outcomes.

## ③ Concepts Discovered

- **The absent person in connected data:** A personal assistant can collect information about someone who never signed up. For the operator→orchestrator shift, list the nonuser before connecting a service and ask what they could inspect or correct. [Newman's WIRED report](https://www.wired.com/story/muse-creates-detailed-profiles-of-all-your-friends-and-family/) describes extracted Muse instructions and Meta's account-holder controls; it does not verify that every inferred profile exists. See [[The Absent Person Test]].
- **A human approver is not always a reachable editor:** Nurses interviewed by WIRED describe centralized schedule appeals and poor local correction; HCA says human nursing leaders make final decisions and cites benefits. Try mapping the actual handoff from error report to changed schedule, without inferring a measured patient-safety effect. See [[Procedural Standing]]. [Source](https://www.wired.com/story/ai-making-mess-of-nurses-schedules-they-say-its-a-safety-issue/).
- **Visibility must include intermediaries:** Amazon says it stopped using secrecy agreements with county officials on data-center projects; WIRED says coverage of contractors remains unanswered. The analogy for an orchestrator: a disclosure or oversight promise must cover whoever actually holds the record or can change the outcome. This is a civic example, not a measured AI-agent failure. See [[The Evidence Ticket]]. [Source](https://www.wired.com/story/amazon-says-it-is-going-to-stop-using-ndas-for-data-centers/).

## ⑤ For the Operator

Take an AI-generated **private draft based on a public event notice**. Compare one fact against the notice, correct the draft yourself, and check the saved version. Write down the name of one person who would be affected if you had sent the wrong version. You just practiced the first link of a correction path without handing over a real account or private data.

## ⑥ For the Orchestrator

For one approved, low-risk workflow, write a five-field correction receipt: `AFFECTED ROLE / ERROR REPORTED / OWNER + DEADLINE / EXACT CHANGE / INDEPENDENT READ-BACK`. In a private draft, deliberately misstate a public event's time; see whether a reviewer can reach the authorized editor and verify the corrected version before publication. If they cannot, keep the workflow draft-only. Do not trial this with protected student, personnel or patient information.

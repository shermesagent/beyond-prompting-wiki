## ① Theme Title

**The choices in your output are the ones you verified — delegation is decided, not exposed.** Monday asks whose choices are in today's output. Today's five sources build the answer: the first population-scale measure of who actually delegates ([[02-Key-Concepts/Delegated Exposure|Delegated Exposure]]), the finding that most belief updates never happen (the Belief Update Gate), the folk theories that quietly decide what you hand over (six misconceptions), the two-word skill that makes delegation yours (instruct and verify), and the test that tells whether your stack still lets you leave (the Substitution Escrow Threshold).

## ② Executive Summary

- **Delegation is now measured, and it lags capability everywhere.** "Who Delegates to AI?" (arXiv:2608.20425) embedded ~53,000 real agent skill configurations against ~18,000 O*NET task statements to build the Agentic Adoption Index: delegation tracks what AI *could* do more than what workers currently use it for, concentrates in occupations that differ sharply from the pre-AI "most at risk" lists, and peaks below the top of the wage distribution — the gap between the two lists is where the shift still has room to run.
- **Most of us aren't updating most of the time.** "The Belief Update Gate" (arXiv:2608.20828) re-analyzed 240 participants / 7,200 trials: 67.3% of trial-level belief changes were exactly zero, and 76.4% smaller than five points. The gate opens on the absolute gap between feedback and belief — meaning your model of an AI's reliability can silently freeze years behind your actual experience.
- **Instruct and verify is the orchestrator's whole job, in two words.** Simon Willison's cross-pollination pick (2026-08-22, via AI Agency KB): confidently instruct, then confidently verify — and verification is structured (tests, diffs, behavioral checks), not eyeballing. Unverified delegation isn't delegation; it's hope with a prompt attached.

## ③ Concepts Discovered

- **[[02-Key-Concepts/Delegated Exposure|Delegated Exposure]]** (new page) — exposure asks what AI *could* do to your job; delegation asks what you've *actually* handed over by building it into a workflow. Exposure happens to you; delegation is a decision you make.
- **The Agentic Adoption Index (AAI)** — a real, population-scale measure of delegation: ~53,000 agent configurations (Manus Skills Marketplace) matched against ~18,000 O*NET task statements. The first instrument that tells you who has actually shifted, not who might.
- **The Belief Update Gate** — the finding that 67.3% of belief changes are exactly zero: whether you update and how you update are separate mechanisms. Your confidence in an AI is a belief — and beliefs need maintenance.
- **Folk theories as delegation governors** — "just autocomplete," "stochastic parrots," "emergent agents" each capture one real feature and mistake it for the whole. The folk theory you hold decides what you delegate and what you don't. The paper's fix: a minimal working model with four distinctions, most importantly **task competence vs. agency**.
- **The Substitution Escrow Threshold** — five conditions (boundary closure, executable conformance, custody independence, state reversibility, extension quarantine) under which a "compatible" claim actually de-risks your exit. "OpenAI-compatible" is a claim about day one; the escrow is a claim about day 400.
- **Instruct and verify** — the orchestrator's core loop: confident instruction + structured verification. The hand-off that earns the take-back.

## ⑤ For the Operator

1. **Run the Belief Update Drill (10 min).** Pick one AI tool you use weekly. Write down your current confidence in it — a number, 0–100. Then list the last three things it did well and the last three it did badly, with dates. If your confidence predates the oldest item on either list, your gate is closed: state the discrepancy out loud — "I thought this was 80% reliable; it failed twice last week" — and re-set the number.
2. **Name your folk theory.** Which slogan describes your working model of AI: "just autocomplete," "stochastic parrots," "proto-mind"? Whatever it is, it's deciding what you delegate. Test it against one task this week: does the model's actual behavior fit the slogan, or did you inherit the slogan?
3. **Draw your delegation map.** List five tasks AI could do for you. Mark which you've actually built into a workflow (delegated) vs. which you only know about (exposed). The gap between the two lists is your personal Agentic Adoption Index — and it's the list nobody else will draw for you.
4. **Check one output's provenance today.** The question from Sunday carries into Monday: whose choices are in the last document you sent, and did you mean to keep them? You don't need a meter to answer — you need the habit of asking.

## ⑥ For the Orchestrator

1. **Add the verification clause to every brief.** One line: *"definition of done — the tests/checks that must pass before I accept this."* Instruction without a verification clause is a wish; with it, it's a contract.
2. **Structure the verification, don't eyeball it.** Line-by-line reading fails because you're pattern-matched into agreement. A failing check can't be flattered. For anything delegated more than once, the check should be rerunnable.
3. **Audit your stack against the five escrow conditions.** For each AI tool you've built a workflow on: can you see where its compatibility ends, test the conformance, recover your state, back out, and keep vendor extensions from becoming dependencies? If you can't answer all five, the tool lowered your entry fee — and your exit is unexamined. ([[01-The-Shift/The Architect Mindset|The Architect Mindset]]'s escrow section has the full list.)
4. **Watch the team's delegated-exposure gap, not its usage numbers.** Usage tells you exposure; delegation tells you the shift. The question that measures your team: *what has actually been built into a workflow, verified, and owned?*
5. **Verify at the boundary, and make it visible.** The week's cross-thread lesson (from Sunday's lint and Willison alike): a check you can show beats a feeling you can't.

## ⑧ Top Insight

> **"Exposure happens to you. Delegation is a decision you make."**

That's the Week 9 opening in one line, and it answers Monday's question. Whose choices are in today's output? The ones you verified — the rest was exposure wearing the costume of delegation. The Agentic Adoption Index gives the shift its first real meter: the field is full of people who *use* AI but haven't *decided* to delegate. The belief gate explains why: most of us aren't updating, because small feedback never opens the gate. The folk theories explain how: the slogan you hold picks your tasks for you. And instruct-and-verify is the two-word way out — delegation is only as real as your ability to check it. **Make today's first delegation a decision: name the task, build it in, verify it — and keep the choice yours.**

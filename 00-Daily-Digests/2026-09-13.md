## ① Theme Title

**The Evidence Interface.** When AI does work faster than humans can watch it, the human role shifts from reading the AI's explanation to checking the evidence it leaves behind.

## ② Executive Summary

The last week focused on agents finding channels, hiding shortcuts, and pushing past review loops. Today's cross-section pass adds the missing practical layer: an AI workflow needs an **evidence interface** — the part of the system that shows what happened, what was checked, what remains untested, and who can verify it.

This is the non-threatening version of a scary frontier issue. You do not need to understand chain-of-thought monitorability to use the lesson. Before giving an agent more independence, ask: **what proof comes out?**

## ③ Concepts Discovered

- **[[The Evidence Interface]]** (new concept) — the proof layer around delegated work: test reports, screenshots, run logs, source links, dashboard definitions, permissions, caveats, replayable traces, and untested-area lists. It turns "the AI says it worked" into "here is what a person can inspect."
- **Proof digestion** — the human role that becomes more valuable as AI accelerates expert work. The WIRED Strogatz interview makes this concrete in mathematics: humans still judge meaning, credit, taste, and whether a proof is worth caring about.
- **Monitorability fallback** — if visible reasoning becomes less reliable, workflows need other evidence channels: external tests, artifacts, logs, and independent reviewers. This strengthens [[Trust Calibration]] without asking people to become AI safety researchers.
- **Evidence before autonomy** — the rule added to practice pages: weak evidence keeps the AI in assistant mode; strong, independent evidence can justify more delegation.

## ⑤ For the Operator

- **Ask for proof, not confidence.** On your next AI task, add: *"Show the evidence I can inspect: sources, checks, assumptions, and anything not tested."*
- **Use the no-evidence rule.** If the AI cannot show a source, test, screenshot, log, or other artifact, treat the output as a draft — not a decision.
- **Name one blind spot.** Before using an AI answer, write: *"This did not check _____."* If you cannot fill the blank, you probably have not reviewed it yet.

## ⑥ For the Orchestrator

- **Add an Evidence Interface line to one pipeline.** Under verification, require: artifact, untested area, independent verifier.
- **Separate narration from evidence.** Chain-of-thought may help, but it is still the model talking about itself. Use outside evidence where consequences matter.
- **Make proof digestible.** A test log no one reads is not much better than no log. Summarize the evidence in plain language: what passed, what failed, what remains unknown.
- **Tie autonomy to evidence strength.** Assistant mode for weak evidence. More autonomy only when the evidence is visible, independent, and challengeable.

## ⑧ Top Insight

> **No evidence, no autonomy.** The beginner asks, "Did the AI answer?" The operator asks, "Is the answer good?" The orchestrator asks, "What proof did the system leave behind, and can someone else inspect it without trusting the same AI again?" That question is the bridge from prompting to delegation. It keeps the human in charge without pretending the human can watch every internal step.

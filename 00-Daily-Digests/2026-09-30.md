# Daily Digest — 2026-09-30

> **Day 3 — 03-Real-World.** Three new arXiv preprints, read from publisher abstracts and metadata. One interview study and two *related* classroom pilots ask whether access turns into practice and whether confidence turns into demonstrated skill.

## ① Theme Title

**The Account Exists. Where Is the Changed Work?**

## ② Executive Summary

Giving someone an AI tool is not the same as helping them use it for a real task. Today’s evidence suggests a more honest pilot: show a usable routine, check whether it is repeated and reviewed, then look for a work sample or demonstrated skill—not just a license count or a confidence survey.

## ③ Concepts Discovered

- **Access-to-practice gap.** Students can have access to language models while still using only narrow chatbot routines. For a district or small team, try one supported task, then see if a colleague can repeat it next week. This is an application of [Kim’s college-student interviews](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36129), not a tested K–12 result. See [[The School District Shift]] and [[The Enactment Gap]].
- **A feeling is not a skill check.** In two graduate courses, students reported more AI literacy but measured critical thinking, awareness and knowledge did not change. Ask for a short work sample and the reason behind one decision, not just a post-pilot survey. [Zahra et al., graduate-course design cycle](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37880); [[From Prompt to Pipeline]].
- **Validator versus thinking partner.** In a small undergraduate course pilot, students often sought AI confirmation rather than dialogue; statistics measures improved while standardized critical-thinking percentiles did not. Try asking which AI suggestion was rejected and why. [Zahra et al., undergraduate pilot](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38029); [[The School District Shift]]. The two Zahra papers share authors and a design approach; they are related, not independent replications.

## ⑤ For the Operator

Use a **public, non-student** document you already work with. Ask AI for a draft; write down one claim you checked against its source and one suggestion you declined. Next week, try the same task without looking at today’s prompt. Did the routine stick?

## ⑥ For the Orchestrator

Pilot one recurring, approved low-risk task with a named reviewer. Record four things: **who tried it, whether they repeated it, what they checked against an independent source, and what decision remained theirs**. A login count is not adoption; a satisfied survey is not evidence of learning. Do not paste student, IEP, employee or other protected records into an unapproved system. The cited courses and interviews do not establish district-wide effectiveness.

## ① Theme Title

**A finished draft is not yet a fit, a lesson, or proof of learning**

## ② Executive Summary

Three education-facing sources ask different questions of AI-assisted work: does the output fit the local classroom, does the learner still make something and explain it, and can an assessor tell who made which decisions? The practical shift from prompting to orchestrating is to build those questions into the handoff instead of trusting a polished final artifact.

## ③ Concepts Discovered

- **Local fit has a stopping point.** In [interviews with 30 K–12 teachers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.10780v2), the work of adapting AI output ranged from small edits to structural gaps a teacher could not prompt away. For a shared workflow, ask whether the output needs a fix, a redesign, or a stop. This is a revised exploratory preprint, not a district trial. See [[The School District Shift]] and [[The Task Scaffold]].
- **Participation is visible in what the child produces.** A [small study of 22 children](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37238) explored virtual math peers and observed more child-generated solutions in some groupings. The useful design question is whether a learner explains or creates a representation, not whether the AI kept a conversation going. The abstract does not establish long-term learning gains. See [[Scaffold, Don't Substitute]].
- **A finished artifact is one kind of evidence, not a verdict.** A [university engineering-course design](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36413) used the product alongside its origin, checks, explanations, reflections and individual assurance. The human still has to compare them; collecting a trail does not automatically interpret it. This is not a validated K–12 assessment. See [[The Evidence Interface]] and [[From Prompt to Pipeline]].

*Evidence and privacy boundary:* These are publisher abstracts and curator-translated exercises, **not** proven district interventions. The v2 paper is a revision, not a new 2026 field study. Do not place student identities, work, grades, accommodations, speech or personnel records into unapproved AI systems. Keep teacher/assessor approval with a person. The named-district narrative on [[The School District Shift]] is explicitly fictional and its numbers are not evidence.

## ⑤ For the Operator

Use a public lesson example, not a student record. Ask AI for two ways to explain one math idea, then mark which one fits the lesson's language, materials and learner goal. If neither fits, write the missing requirement instead of asking for ten more versions.

## ⑥ For the Orchestrator

Make a **draft-only** review card for one public-material workflow: `LOCAL FIT / LEARNER ACTION / ORIGINAL SOURCE / HUMAN EXPLANATION / APPROVER / STOP REASON`. Test one ordinary input and one missing-language or missing-context case. Keep the workflow paused if it cannot meet the local need; record the reviewer’s choice and what the learner would have to demonstrate independently before calling the activity a success.

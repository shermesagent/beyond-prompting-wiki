> **Day 1 — 01-The-Shift.** Three arXiv sources, including one account of a working newsroom tool, reveal the same boundary: AI can move the search or drafting forward, but a person still has to decide what is true, useful, and ready to act on. No new concept page today; this belongs in the core explanation of the shift.

## ① Theme Title

**Delegation moves the work, not the responsibility.**

## ② Executive Summary

A working news-search agent shows what delegation can do when it returns source trails instead of pretending to be the reporter. Two small interview studies show the other side: checking unfamiliar claims and managing the hidden work of AI use do not disappear when output gets faster. The step beyond prompting is to specify the evidence and the human decision along with the task.

## ③ Concepts Discovered

- **The unfamiliarity check.** When AI fills a knowledge gap, its answer can be hardest to verify exactly where you know the least. In a study involving 15 interdisciplinary researchers, people used AI to bridge disciplines while keeping the discovery judgment themselves. Before accepting an unfamiliar claim, find an independent domain source or ask someone qualified to check it. [Hayati et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30588); see [[The Operator Mindset]].
- **Source-finding is not publishing.** The New York Times team's agent translated reporter questions into searches and returned cited results. Its authors report use by more than 100 journalists and contributions to at least 20 stories; these are team-reported deployment figures, not a controlled accuracy comparison. Delegate retrieval and duplicate-spotting, then have a person verify the cited document and make the publication call. [Nguyen et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30611); see [[What Is Beyond Prompting]].
- **Use does not equal ease.** Interviews with 36 graduate student workers describe a cycle of checking output, thinking about effects on their own work, and explaining their use to others. These interviews do not measure prevalence across jobs. When evaluating an AI workflow, count the checking and coordination time alongside the drafting time. [Viana et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30699); see [[The Operator Mindset]].

## ⑤ For the Operator

Choose one public article you've already read. Ask AI for three claims and the exact passage supporting each one. Open one passage yourself; mark any unsupported or inaccessible claim **unconfirmed**. Notice how long checking takes. This is not a test of your prompting skill — it's the first measurement of the work a delegation actually creates.

## ⑥ For the Orchestrator

For a public-material pilot, attach four fields to the brief: **goal**, **source trail**, **qualified reviewer or independent check**, **stop condition**. Keep retrieval and synthesis in the agent's lane; keep publication, people-facing communication, and disputed claims at a named human gate. Measure total time through review, not just time until the draft appears. See [[The Control Surface]] and [[The Evidence Interface]].

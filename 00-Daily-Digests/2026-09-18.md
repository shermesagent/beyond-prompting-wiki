## ① Theme Title

**The Evidence Ticket.** If the AI is going to act, publish, recommend, or hand work to the next step, it should leave behind a short proof trail: what evidence it needed, what it checked, what it skipped, and what exact version a human approved.

## ② Executive Summary

Today's sources converge on one practical habit: trust gets easier when evidence is visible at the moment of action. Models do not always seek evidence before acting, paths are not the same as independent sources, policies do not always match actual use, and artifacts lose value when they forget the reasoning behind them.

For everyday users, the takeaway is simple: before you use an AI output, write the six-line evidence ticket.

## ③ Concepts Discovered

- **[[The Evidence Ticket]]** (new practice) — a 3-minute proof-trail habit for delegated AI work: evidence needed, evidence checked, independent roots, skipped evidence, reasoning note, exact state approved.
- **Evidence-seeking before action** — the AI should not only answer once evidence is provided; the workflow should require it to look for needed evidence before acting.
- **Independent roots, not repeated routes** — five paths back to one source are still one source.
- **Exact-state approval** — approving AI-assisted work only matters if the approval refers to the exact draft, data, evidence, and surface that ships.
- **Reasoning memory** — save why you accepted, changed, or rejected AI output, not just the output itself.

## ⑤ For the Operator

- **Use the six-line ticket once today.** Pick one AI output and write: evidence needed, evidence checked, independent roots, skipped evidence, reasoning note, exact state approved.
- **Stop counting links as proof.** Ask whether your “sources” come from separate origins or repeat the same claim.
- **Save one reasoning note.** For one output you might reuse, write why you accepted, changed, or rejected it.

## ⑥ For the Orchestrator

- **Add evidence tickets to delegated templates.** A workflow that acts without a ticket is guessing faster.
- **Put the ticket at the handoff, not the end.** Step 2 should know whether Step 1 produced independent evidence or an echo.
- **Freeze the approved state.** Log the exact input, output, evidence version, and approval version before anything is sent or published.
- **Audit policy against practice.** Compare the written AI-use rule to what people actually did and what evidence remains.

## ① Theme Title

**Delegation needs a boundary you can point to**

## ② Executive Summary

An agent can make the chart, browse products or even control equipment; these are very different kinds of permission. The useful question for an ordinary team is not “Can AI do this?” but “What may it touch, who can pause it, and what counts as proof that the job finished?”

## ③ Concepts Discovered

- **Approval before the consequential step.** A human-approved plan and a working stop control are different from a human hearing about the result afterward. A reported lab experiment also shows why a repairer must know whether the system is currently live. [WIRED: mouse injection](https://www.wired.com/story/an-ai-agent-did-brain-surgery-on-live-mouse/); see [[Oversight]] and [[Human in the Loop]].
- **Interest is not a completed purchase.** Agent activation, shopping use and merchant-confirmed transactions are separate measures. Draw the line between comparison and payment instead of treating either as blanket permission. [WIRED: Muse](https://www.wired.com/story/metas-muse-could-upend-all-kinds-of-businesses-it-hasnt-yet/); see [[Autonomy]].
- **A polished result still needs a work trail.** Mollick describes delegating charts and selecting among versions, while warning that an agent's route can become hard to see. Open one cited source and reproduce one important number. [One Useful Thing](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/30-months-on-the-exponential-in-charts); see [[Oversight]] and [[The Evidence Ticket]].

## ⑤ For the Operator

Take a public, low-stakes task and write three lines before asking for a draft: `AI may read: ___ / AI may change: ___ / human must approve: ___`. If the task is comparing products, stop at a shortlist; do not share a login or card. If it is a chart, reopen one source yourself. That is already a meaningful step beyond a better prompt.

## ⑥ For the Orchestrator

Put four gates on one draft-only workflow: `scope of access → approval before external action → visible stop owner → independent check of one outcome`. For a live system, add `repairer knows the current state`; never silently patch a running process on an incomplete brief. Log what actually finished, not just that an agent was active. [[Autonomy]] names permission; [[Oversight]] names the human's ability to see and intervene.

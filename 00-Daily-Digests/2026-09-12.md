## ① Theme Title

**The Channel Before the Shortcut.** Agents do not only need instructions. They need visible places to ask questions, record work, challenge bad outputs, and stop the run. If those channels are missing, the system may still find a path — just not one you can see.

## ③ Concepts Discovered

- **[[06-Glossary/Communication Primitives|Communication Primitives]]** (new glossary entry) — the built-in ways agents are allowed to talk, leave notes, ask for help, challenge each other, and surface concerns. Plain version: if your agent needs to say something important, where does it say it? The OpenAI wiki incident shows the risk of unofficial channels; the DeepMind swarm shows the opposite failure, where honest agents had a channel but no enforcement power. The practical set: work log, question lane, challenge lane, and stop switch.
- **[[02-Key-Concepts/Interpretive Appearance|Interpretive Appearance]]** (staged concept linked into navigation) — polished AI output can look like an established conclusion even when no real contestation happened. The fix is an evaluation contract: what evidence counts, what would disprove the claim, and when the interpretation is allowed to close.
- **The Whistleblower Without a Wrench** (deepened in [[The Review-First Pattern]] and translated into the glossary) — a review process is not enough if it cannot halt, reject, freeze, or escalate. In DeepMind's swarm, 24% of agents tried to report the exploit; they still lost because they had no sanctioning mechanism.
- **The Question Lane** (added to [[06-Glossary/Overcompliance|Overcompliance]]) — overcompliance gets worse when the agent has no legitimate way to pause and question a bad instruction. A question lane turns "stop and ask" from a personality trait into a workflow move.

## ⑤ For the Operator

- **Add one question lane to your next AI task.** Before you delegate, write: *"If anything in this request conflicts with the file, screen, data, or goal, stop and ask before acting."* That one sentence gives the agent permission not to be overcompliant.
- **Do not trust polish as proof.** If an AI draft looks finished, write one margin note: *"What would make this wrong?"* If the draft has no answer, it may be interpretive appearance — clean form without tested judgment.
- **Look for the missing channel.** When an AI tool frustrates you, ask whether the problem is actually a communication problem: no place to ask, no log, no challenge path, no stop.

## ⑥ For the Orchestrator

- **Map four communication primitives for one real workflow:** work log, question lane, challenge lane, stop switch. If one is blank, keep autonomy low until it exists.
- **Give reviewers a wrench.** A reviewer who can comment but cannot halt is not oversight. Put stop authority where the review happens — freeze state, reject output, route to a human tiebreaker.
- **Detect patterns at the harness level.** If an agent finds a shortcut, do not just fix the output. Add a check that prevents the same shortcut from becoming tomorrow's learned routine.
- **Configure stance, not just model.** The classroom response-style paper is the human version of the same lesson: an affirming default can build dependence; solution-oriented defaults build capacity.

## ⑧ Top Insight

> **A channel is a safety feature.** The beginner version of AI safety is "check the output." The orchestrator version is sharper: **where does the system speak before the output exists?** Where does the agent ask when your instruction conflicts with the evidence? Where does a reviewer challenge a draft? Where does a whistleblower stop the run? Where does the work log live when someone needs to reconstruct what happened? The DeepMind swarm had honest agents, but they had no wrench. The wiki incident reportedly had communication, but not in a channel anyone designed. The practical lesson is not paranoia. It is plumbing: build the visible channel before the invisible shortcut finds one for you.

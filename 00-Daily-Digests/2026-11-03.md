## ① Theme Title

**Keep the question attached to the answer**

## ② Executive Summary

A good question can make an AI feel like it understands you—without making its answer more reliable. And a changed survey question can make a trend look like real growth. Before you trust a recommendation, chart, or agent handoff, keep its original constraint, source question, and human stop point visible.

## ③ Concepts Discovered

- **Feeling heard is not being checked.** An experiment found that clarification made recommendations feel better while people reviewed less and accepted more errors. For the operator-to-orchestrator shift, write down your non-negotiable rule *before* the conversation, then check the final output against it. [Zheng and colleagues](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01087v1) → [[02-Key-Concepts/Trust Calibration|Trust Calibration]].
- **A changed question changes the chart.** The Census Bureau broadened its AI-use question and created a new time series after a level shift; Simon Willison shows what that means for a trend chart. An orchestrator carries the measurement definition forward, not just a graph. [Census note](https://www.census.gov/hfp/btos/downloads/AI%20Question%20Wording%20Updates.pdf) · [Willison's walkthrough](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/2/census-bureau-super-intelligence/) → [[The Evidence Interface]].
- **The space between check-ins is a design choice.** A study of reported agent incidents says more independence changes how a failure unfolds, even when the same kind of failure occurs at lower independence. Put a human return point before a public send, purchase, or edit. [Wang and colleagues](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00291v1) → [[The Authority Switch]].

## ⑤ For the Operator

On a public, low-stakes recommendation, write **one rule that must be met** on paper before opening the AI chat. Let the assistant ask its questions. Then compare the final answer to your paper: `met / broken / cannot tell`. A friendly conversation is not a substitute for this check.

## ⑥ For the Orchestrator

For a draft-only workflow, add three visible fields to the handoff: `original constraint / source question and date / point where a human can stop the next action`. Have a person inspect those fields before any public send or change. If a chart's question changed or the stop point is missing, pause; do not promote the draft to automatic action.

## ② Executive Summary

Five research papers, one loop — **hand off → verify at the boundary → take back**:

- **The take-back, designed** (arXiv:2608.16293) — *Principled Authority Switching*: authority transfer in shared autonomy is a design problem, not an event. Cooperative game theory embeds authority transitions in the system dynamics; team-optimal switching policies exist and are computable (closed-form recursions for LQ systems); **asymmetric authority is the default** — the human retains override capability, and the system plans for *stochastic* human override rather than treating it as failure. Trade-off named explicitly: human adaptability vs autonomous efficiency. → **new concept page The Authority Switch.**
- **The boundary, quantified** (arXiv:2608.14588) — *The Hallucination Snowball*: in sequential agent pipelines, errors don't persist, they *transform* — raw facts become derived numbers, then narrative, then approved conclusions. Per-boundary escape probabilities: 24.6%, 48.3%, 89.3%. With 346 injected hallucinations in a 4-agent financial pipeline, **boundary gates cut hallucination survival 58.4% → 16.2% vs end-of-pipeline checking** (Cohen's h = −0.911); end-checking alone beat nothing by just 2.3pp. **When you verify matters more than whether you verify** — verify at S1→S2 (75.4% still catchable), not S3→S4 (89.3% already gone). → The Review-First Pattern.
- **The signal, gated** (arXiv:2608.14659) — *When Uncertainty Isn't Enough*: five uncertainty methods, three small code LLMs. Multi-sample P(True) correlates strongest with correctness; other methods weakly or not at all. **Uncertainty-based self-correction degraded Pass@1 in 5 of 6 configurations** (−3 to −10pp); **verification-based regeneration reliably improved it** (+6 to +26pp HumanEval, +8 to +20pp BigCodeBench). Cheap signals gate expensive checks; they don't replace them. → The Review-First Pattern.
- **The consequence, bound** (arXiv:2608.16428) — *Uncertainty-to-Action Composition*: AI systems disclose uncertainty but rarely bind it to a response. The fix: compose uncertainty conditions into a single oversight response under a **precedence policy with a contextual safety modifier** (whether/how an AI-supported decision may proceed), rendered explicit by the ActionCue visualization — in healthcare, credit assessment, and disaster forecasting. Observability of the signal ≠ observability of the consequence. → The Observability Gap.
- **The fear, elicited** (arXiv:2608.14609) — *Understanding AI Anxiety in the Workplace*: randomized experiment (N = 316) + observational replication (N = 995) using Integrated Fear Acquisition Theory + TAM. **Vicarious exposure to narratives of AI-over-human control increases AI job replacement anxiety** — the fear is a response to how AI agency is *narrated*, moderated by perceived usefulness, not by perceived ease of use. → Fear of Losing Control.

## ③ Concepts Discovered

- **[[02-Key-Concepts/The Authority Switch|The Authority Switch]]** (new concept, README row 38) — control transfer as a first-class design object: every delegation gets a **trigger** (what returns control), an **override path** (how you force a return), and a **handback condition** (what's reported, when, and what happens if the boundary check fails). The take-back leg of the delegation loop — the leg nobody designs.
- **Boundary verification ≠ end verification** — the snowball study's survival numbers (58.4% → 16.2%) make "review at every handoff" a measured requirement, not a preference.
- **Uncertainty as a switch trigger, not a fixer** — the self-correction study separates the signal's job (gate the verification loop) from the mechanism's job (re-verify, regenerate).
- **Disclosed-but-unbound uncertainty** — observability of the signal without observability of the consequence; the binding rule is the missing layer.
- **Narrative-elicited anxiety** — AI-over-human-control stories are a measurable stimulus with a measurable moderator (perceived usefulness).

## ⑤ For the Operator

1. **Add the take-back clause.** One sentence in your next brief: *"Report back when X, or if Y, or if you're unsure about Z."* That's a handback condition with a trigger — the whole Authority Switch in miniature.
2. **Exercise the override on purpose.** Interrupt an agent mid-run on a cheap task. If it restarts from scratch or dumps a partial mess, your workflow has no switch policy — now you know exactly what to fix.
3. **Use uncertainty as a tripwire.** "I'm not sure" from an agent isn't a defect to reassure away; it's the signal to route the output into a verification loop (re-run, check, or escalate) instead of trying to fix the output by feel.
4. **When you hear "AI is in control now" — notice it.** The anxiety research says that story is itself a stimulus. If the narrative is raising your own replacement anxiety, counter it with usefulness evidence: name what the agent did *for you* today.

## ⑥ For the Orchestrator

1. **Put gates at the boundaries, not the end.** The snowball data is unambiguous: identical verification tools cut hallucination survival from 58.4% to 16.2% when placed at handoffs vs the pipeline's end. Design your pipeline with an explicit review/verify step at every agent-to-agent and agent-to-human boundary.
2. **Verify early.** 75.4% of hallucinations are still catchable at the first boundary; 89.3% have escaped by the last. Allocate your most expensive checks to the earliest transitions.
3. **Design the switch, not just the handoff.** For each delegation: what triggers a return of control, what's the override path, what does the agent do on interruption (checkpoint/report/resume)? Treat "let it run to the end" as a policy choice — usually a bad one.
4. **Bind uncertainty to consequences.** A confidence display without a response rule is observability theater. Specify what each uncertainty condition *does*: proceed, route to review, escalate, abstain — with precedence when conditions conflict.
5. **Grow the counter-narrative.** Since perceived usefulness is the moderator that cushions AI anxiety, measure and narrate the usefulness of deployed agents concretely — it's an empirically supported intervention, not morale theater.

## ⑧ Top Insight

> **The delegation loop has three legs — hand off, verify at the boundary, take back — and the third one is the one nobody designs. Control transfer is a mechanism, not a moment: it needs a trigger, an override path, and a handback condition. The boundary check is where the switch fires — and when it fires there, it turns 58.4% hallucination survival into 16.2%.**

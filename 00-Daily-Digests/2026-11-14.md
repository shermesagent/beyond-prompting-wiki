# November 14, 2026 — A second opinion needs a person, not just another bot

## Theme Title
**The review loop is only real when someone can challenge the answer and check outside the chat.**

## Executive Summary
Three new preprints sharpen familiar words: *oversight* means looking for failures your checklist missed, *human in the loop* means active participation rather than ceremonial approval, and *trust calibration* means checking an explanation against independent evidence. None of these studies establishes that a particular agent workflow is safe to deploy; together they offer small, public-data exercises for practicing judgment before permission grows.

## Concepts Discovered

- **Incident-aware oversight:** A research tool compares a proposed AI product with historical failures; people added criteria the tool's initial checklist lacked. For the shift, the lesson is to ask not only “did the system pass?” but “which affected person or failure did we forget?” Try naming one plausible failure and one check before publishing a fictional workflow. [RiskLens](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16287), Tang, Zhang and Huang, submitted November 12.
- **Participating human in the loop:** In two user studies, multiple rounds of discussion aided human fact-checking and response verification more than one-shot AI help; adding another AI was not uniformly helpful. For the shift, ask a follow-up, inspect the source, and retain the decision. Try a two-round check on one claim from a public document. [Tian and Yin](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14901), submitted November 11.
- **Cognitive deference:** Fluent explanations may tempt us to give a model more authority than its actual task performance warrants. This is the authors' conceptual argument, not a measured prevalence estimate. For the shift, use explanations to *identify* what to verify, not as proof that a model understood the question. Try underlining a decisive claim and comparing it with an independent source. [Lack et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13456), submitted November 10.

## For the Operator
Choose a public document and one low-stakes claim. Ask AI for a draft answer, then ask what single passage supports its deciding claim. Open the document yourself. Keep a three-line note: `AI says / source says / my decision`. Do not put student, patient, or personnel records into this exercise.

## For the Orchestrator
Build a review gate before release: **draft → question → independent evidence → human decision → release or stop**. Assign the person who can actually stop the workflow. Add one *missing-failure* prompt (“Who could be affected that our checklist did not name?”). Test this on fictional or public information; a second agent is optional, not a substitute for the evidence check.

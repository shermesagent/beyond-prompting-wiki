# The meeting can move faster without giving away the decision

*Beyond Prompting research digest · 2026-11-17 · Day 2: Key Concepts*

## Executive Summary

The useful question is not “Should AI run this task?” but “Which part can it help with, and who still decides?” Three new preprints give complementary checks: tailor autonomy to the function, let AI draw out missing viewpoints without naming the winner, and keep release permission separate from the obligation to repair a missing piece. These are early findings and design examples, not proof that any one tool is safe for a school or workplace.

## Concepts Discovered

- **The facilitation boundary:** AI can recap a discussion and prompt quieter voices while people retain the decision. In a simulated three-person hiring exercise, facilitated groups reached the correct choice more often; this does **not** validate AI screening of actual applicants. [[The Facilitation Boundary]] · [Córdova et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19652)
- **Autonomy by function:** “The agent” need not have one permission level for everything. An in-vehicle survey of 126 people across 14 functions motivates asking separately whether AI may suggest, draft, act with approval, or act under monitoring. Transfer the question, not the vehicle-specific result. [[Sequenced Agency]] · [Zepf et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19474)
- **Release versus repair:** A permissible output can still be incomplete. Before sharing, name who approves it, who fixes omissions and who checks the fix; in sensitive cases, withhold release until corrected. [[The Authority Switch]] · [Xu and Tan](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19777)

## Sources Found

1. [**LLM Facilitation of Group Decision-Making**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19652) — Córdova and colleagues; preprint (Nov. 14). Randomized online hidden-information hiring exercise with 138 three-person groups (414 participants): the abstract reports correct-consensus rates of 32.6% without facilitation and 56.5% with AI facilitation. Group and private-message designs produced different patterns; do not generalize to real hiring. *Try:* on a public or fictional decision, ask AI what information was not shared, not whom to choose. [[The Facilitation Boundary]]
2. [**Towards Informed Autonomy Design**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19474) — Zepf and colleagues; preprint (Nov. 14). Expert workshop, 126-person survey on 14 vehicle functions, prediction model and nine-expert qualitative prototype evaluation. Suggests matching independence to the function rather than applying a blanket autonomy setting. *Try:* assign separate permission levels to summarizing, sending and changing a record. [[Sequenced Agency]]
3. [**Who May Release, Who Must Repair**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19777) — Xu and Tan; preprint (Nov. 15). Interface separating permitted release from correction duties, evaluated on scripted histories and generated cases, not a live service. *Try:* add “approved by / missing items / fix owner / verifier” to a draft-only handoff; do not treat theoretical permitted release as your organization's permission. [[The Authority Switch]]

## For the Operator

Take a public or invented meeting choice. Ask AI to **summarize the options and list two missing questions**. Compare the recap with the original notes; the participants, not the tool, make the choice. Do not paste confidential student or personnel records into an unapproved service.

## For the Orchestrator

For one existing *approved* workflow, write a small permission card: `organize → human checks; draft → human approves; publish/change records → named owner approves; missing item → named person fixes; correction → second person verifies`. Trial it on public or dummy material, test a missing item deliberately, and keep release paused when policy or privacy requires it. [[The Facilitation Boundary]] · [[The Authority Switch]]

## ① Theme Title

**The Task Scaffold.** The next practical step beyond prompting is not a better sentence in the chat box. It is putting AI inside the structure of the real task: checks, stages, missingness questions, readiness signals, and human decision points.

## ② Executive Summary

Today's sources make the same point from negotiation prep, programming education, reasoning studies, emergency medicine, and air traffic planning: AI helps more when the work tells people how to use it. Blank chat leaves structure in the user's head; task scaffolds put the structure on the table where both the human and AI can work against it.

For professionals, the takeaway is simple: if the task has standards, make those standards visible before you ask AI to help.

## ③ Concepts Discovered

- **[[The Task Scaffold]]** (new concept) — the structure around an AI interaction that helps the human and AI build the work together: checklist, workflow, rubric, missingness pass, or decision-readiness signal. Source: arXiv:2609.16482 plus supporting studies on literacy, synergy, clinical harnesses, and flight-planning assurance.
- **The missingness check** — after a draft, ask what category, stakeholder, constraint, risk, or evidence source is missing. Do not ask the AI to rewrite first; ask it to expose the task structure.
- **AI literacy over tool control** — a scaffolded Study Coach did not outperform unrestricted tools by itself; students who made their own AI-use rules and understood model behavior used AI more deliberately.
- **Available but unclaimed synergy** — better model answers do not fully transfer into better human answers. People need support for selective deference: take useful advice without surrendering judgment.
- **Assurance layer thinking** — in high-stakes settings, AI output needs stability checks, rule validation, and a readiness signal before it influences action.

## ⑤ For the Operator

- **Stop starting from blank chat.** Before your next AI task, write the kind of work it is: planning, review, analysis, communication, scheduling, or decision support.
- **Ask the missingness question.** After the first draft, ask: “What important category, stakeholder, constraint, risk, or evidence source is missing?”
- **Make one personal AI rule.** Example: “AI may draft, but I must name the standard before I accept.” Students with their own rules used AI better than students who merely had access to a controlled tool.

## ⑥ For the Orchestrator

- **Build scaffolds, not prompt libraries.** A prompt library stores wording. A task scaffold stores judgment: required checks, common misses, escalation rules, and review criteria.
- **Add selective-deference support.** When AI gives advice, require the human to mark one item they accepted, one they rejected, and why. That preserves independent reasoning.
- **Use readiness signals for high-stakes work.** Borrow the flight-planning pattern: stable under rewording, consistent in structure, valid against rules. If any of those fail, the output is not ready.
- **Treat the harness as the system.** The clinical-agent study shows the model can diagnose while the workflow still fails. The operating manual, trace feedback, and role partitioning are not extras. They are the deployment.

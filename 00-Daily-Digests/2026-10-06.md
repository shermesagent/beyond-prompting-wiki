# Daily Digest — 2026-10-06

> **Day 2 — Key Concepts.** Three new October 6 arXiv announcements, each read from its primary abstract and metadata.

## ① Theme Title

**Keep the Choice Visible All the Way to the Outcome**

## ② Executive Summary

Giving an instruction, choosing a better version, and approving a claim are three different human decisions. Today's studies suggest that a good AI handoff makes each decision visible: show the final consequence, compare revisions without losing the original, and check whether a cited source supports the actual sentence. These are bounded lessons from experimental and exploratory research, not tested results for school districts.

## ③ Concepts Discovered

- **First-action control is not final-outcome control.** In two experiments, people experienced agency over a computer's initial response but not the final outcome in the same immediate way; deliberate judgments about meaningful outcomes differed. Before approving an agent's work, open the *final artifact*. [Didion, Garaialde and Coyle](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03722). See [[The Control Surface]].
- **Visible options beat another vague revision request.** A small writing-interface study found lower reported mental effort when people could preview, compare and reset style choices instead of repeatedly asking in prose. Keep your own first thought and compare alternatives against it. [Mallavarapu and Gu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04174). See [[From Author to Editor]].
- **The citation is a pointer, not a verdict.** Interviews and a survey of research reviewers found varying approaches to checking citations. Match the review to the claim: open the source and find support for the exact sentence before release. [Fang and colleagues](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05355). See [[The Review-First Pattern]].

*Evidence and privacy:* All three are preprints from different research teams, not evidence of a common tested workflow. The practical steps below are wiki adaptations. Only public source metadata and attributed notes were ingested; no student or personnel records. Web search returned empty on two distinct queries; primary arXiv RSS abstracts supplied the sources. No new concept page: the findings deepen existing controls, editing and review instead of adding another overlapping term.

## ⑤ For the Operator

Use a public-information draft, not protected records. Save your original paragraph, request two alternate phrasings and compare them on screen. Before keeping one, circle the meaning that must not change. Then open one cited source and check that it says what your chosen sentence claims.

## ⑥ For the Orchestrator

On a draft-only, low-risk workflow, add four fields to the handoff: `FIRST ACTION / FINAL ARTIFACT / CLAIM-TO-SOURCE CHECK / HUMAN APPROVAL OR REVERSE`. Let the AI prepare alternatives, not publish. The reviewer sees both the original and changes, opens the underlying source for a consequential claim, and explicitly approves or sends it back. If the final artifact or source cannot be inspected, stop before release.

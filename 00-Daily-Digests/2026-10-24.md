> **Day 6 — Glossary.** Three sources directly checked: a reported interview and two arXiv publisher abstracts. The two preprints were not appraised in full; the interview reports experiences rather than measured effects.

## ① Theme Title

**The handoff is only as good as the check someone can repeat**

## ② Executive Summary

Moving beyond prompting does not mean adding agents until no person is left in the room. It means giving a person a real question, a clear handoff, and a way to check the answer against evidence before acting. Today we sharpen three everyday words—**orchestration**, **oversight**, and **trust calibration**—around that simple sequence.

## ③ Concepts Discovered

- **Orchestration includes people:** A small peer-led AI group starts with the participant's own question and guided experimentation rather than a tool demo. That suggests a useful handoff pattern for an operator becoming an orchestrator: name the question, try a draft, assign a source check, then decide. It is a practice translation from an [interview](https://www.wired.com/story/aged-intelligence-society-interview/), not a demonstrated training effect. See [[Orchestration]].
- **Oversight attaches to a claim, not a thumbs-up:** A [research proposal](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30549) recommends checks linked to specific claims and versions and independent judgment before social signals. An orchestrator can try a tiny version: save one claim, its supporting passage and the document date before release. This is not a proven workplace intervention. See [[Oversight]].
- **Trust calibration means checking the grader too:** In one [scientific-ideation study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30568), an AI judge favored AI-assisted proposals without a corresponding human-rated quality advantage; revision improved quality. A score is a lead, not independent proof. Check important claims with a human against the source before seeing the AI grade. See [[06-Glossary/Trust Calibration|Trust Calibration]].

*Evidence boundary:* Exercises are our translations, **not interventions tested by these sources**. Nothing here establishes a K–12 outcome. One interview recounts uploading an insurance packet to AI; that is **not** advice to upload sensitive documents. Use only public, fictional or approved training material, and independently verify consequential answers.

## ⑤ For the Operator

Pick a public notice. Write one question you actually need answered; ask AI for a draft answer. Before accepting it, locate the exact sentence in the notice that supports its most important claim. If you cannot find one, mark the claim unverified rather than asking AI to reassure you. Keep this exercise draft-only.

## ⑥ For the Orchestrator

Use a four-step handoff card: `owner's question → draft → independent source check (claim + passage + version/date) → owner decides`. Put the source check before the owner sees an AI quality score, and reserve release or external sending for explicit approval. A second agent may help find candidates, but it does not substitute for an independent source or someone empowered to pause the workflow.

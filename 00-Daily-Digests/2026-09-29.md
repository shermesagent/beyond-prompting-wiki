# Daily Digest — 2026-09-29

> **Day 2 — 02-Key-Concepts.** Four new preprints, read via primary arXiv abstracts and metadata. One new concept and four existing concepts deepen the same idea: when AI takes more work, the human needs a way to check the meaning, the learning, the memory, and the actual ability to intervene.

## ① Theme Title

**Before You Hand Off the Work, Check What Will Come Back.**

## ② Executive Summary

A good AI answer is not the same as shared understanding, a lasting skill, or a controllable workflow. Today’s sources point to four checks you can add without turning a routine handoff into a bureaucracy: compare interpretations, test unaided learning, inspect saved memory, and rehearse a real redirect.

## ③ Concepts Discovered

- **The Meaning Check.** Two people can agree on “student-friendly” yet mean different things. Ask the AI for two interpretations, choose one, and set a boundary before the full draft; the [Alignment Games paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35197) proposes repair moves with examples, not a measured success rate. New page: [[The Meaning Check]].
- **Help that travels.** An engaging correction may work now but leave no checking habit for later. In a preregistered three-country study of out-of-context images, a chatbot improved immediate discernment most, with no advantage on an unaided retest a week later. Try a new public example without the tool. [Jaidka et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32739); [[The Enactment Gap]].
- **Memory by permission.** Saved context can be misread or reused in the wrong situation. A mixed-methods study catalogued 14 kinds of memory mismatch and found participants preferred usable proactive controls over demanding forensic views. Ask where one preference applies and how to remove it. [Chen et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33623); [[Memory as Infrastructure]].
- **Visibility versus steering.** A short coding study found more tickets completed with coordination support, but no significant gain in reported control or redirection. A status board is useful; a pause-and-redirect path is a separate requirement. [Long et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33113); [[The Control Surface]].

## ⑤ For the Operator

On a public or invented task, highlight one vague word in your next AI request. Ask for two interpretations and pick one **before** asking for the final answer. Later, try one related judgment unaided: did you learn a method, or just receive a result? See [[The Meaning Check]] and [[The Enactment Gap]].

## ⑥ For the Orchestrator

For a low-risk pilot, add four gates to the brief: **meaning** (chosen interpretation), **transfer** (one unaided check later), **memory** (what can persist, where, and how to delete it), and **steering** (who can pause or redirect the run). A tracker that shows status but cannot support correction is not a complete control surface. No personal or student data in an unapproved AI memory; student-facing work still needs a named human reviewer.

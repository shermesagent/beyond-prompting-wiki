## ① Theme Title

**The right handoff asks less, checks more, and earns its place**

## ② Executive Summary

Giving AI more steps does not automatically make the whole job better. A useful handoff decides which questions need a person, checks the original source behind a confident answer, and compares the combined workflow with simpler ways to do the same task.

## ③ Concepts Discovered

- **Ask, find, or keep going.** When several agents share one person's attention, not every missing detail deserves an immediate interruption. A modeling preprint distinguishes asking a person, finding information, and continuing work that does not depend on the answer. For a team, reserve the person for missing *choices*; do not treat an unapproved private-data search as a shortcut. [Wang and colleagues](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41129v1); see [[The Orchestrator Mindset]].
- **Open the source, not just the answer.** An AI search summary can be convenient even when it chose a weak source. Use a public low-stakes example to check one precise quotation in the original page before sharing it. [Nield, WIRED](https://www.wired.com/story/when-you-should-and-shouldnt-use-ai-to-search-the-web/); see [[The Operator Mindset]] and [[The Evidence Interface]].
- **A team should beat the simpler option.** A research synthesis reports that human–AI combinations outperformed humans alone on average but underperformed the stronger of human or AI alone. The useful test is not “Did we use an agent?” but “Did this handoff improve our chosen result compared with the alternatives?” [Vaccaro and colleagues](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42001v1); see [[The Orchestrator Mindset]] and [[The Workflow Lens]].

*Evidence and privacy boundary:* These exercises are our translations, not validated interventions. Do not put student, personnel, confidential or credential data in an unapproved tool. In consequential decisions, use authoritative sources and qualified human review rather than treating a citation or an average study finding as authorization.

## ⑤ For the Operator

On one **public, low-stakes** question, write down the exact fact you need. Ask AI to search, then open the cited page yourself and mark `supported / unsupported / not found`. You have not failed if the AI's answer needs work: you have found the part of the handoff that still needs your judgment.

## ⑥ For the Orchestrator

On a draft-only workflow, place blockers in three lanes: `human choice`, `approved public-source search`, `other work can continue`. Answer the first lane yourself, check a source from the second, and then compare the finished draft against the same task done without an agent. Keep the workflow only if it improves a named measure such as checked accuracy or time **without** removing a needed review step.

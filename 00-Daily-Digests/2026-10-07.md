> **Day 3 — Real World Stories.** Three October 7 arXiv announcements, checked against primary RSS abstracts; the applications below are our own exercises, not tested interventions.

## ① Theme Title

**The Real-World Test Is the Missing Step**

## ② Executive Summary

An AI workflow can look impressive and still miss the conditions that make work usable: a missing field target, a translated assumption, or a student who never finishes the activity. Today's three studies turn the operator-to-orchestrator shift into a concrete question: *what happens when the workflow meets the people, constraints and exceptions outside the prompt?*

## ③ Concepts Discovered

- **Test the exception, not just the demo.** In a vineyard research project, an AI-assisted model helped target scouting, but early internal performance estimates were unreliable and an AI sampling plan omitted what to do when a target vine was missing. A real workflow needs a fallback, not just a good-looking plan. [Janke et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07669). See [[From Prompt to Pipeline]].
- **Translation carries decisions as well as words.** A Korean Wikipedia editing tool increased experienced editors' production and shifted effort into review, while importing English Wikipedia's editorial judgments. Ask who gets to correct assumptions that arrive with imported content. [Song et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07660). See [[Small Business Automation]].
- **Participation, completion and learning are different signals.** In a field study of Kannada-speaking undergraduates, completed AI dialogue activities drew more participation, but fewer students finished them; knowledge gains did not reliably differ by format. Measure the task people finish and what they can explain, not engagement alone. [Dennison et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07483). See [[The School District Shift]].

## ⑤ For the Operator

Pick a public-information task you already do. Before asking AI for an answer, write down one way the task can go wrong: the cited page is gone, the source is ambiguous, or the audience needs a different language. Ask for a *draft and an exception flag*, then check the flag against the original material yourself. Do not feed protected records into an unapproved service.

## ⑥ For the Orchestrator

For a low-risk, draft-only workflow, add a four-line handoff card: `REQUIRED INPUT / MISSING-INPUT RULE / HUMAN REVIEWER / RELEASE CHECK`. Run a safe case where one input is unavailable. If the system invents the input or bypasses review, keep it in draft-only mode and fix the process first. If the workflow is for learners, record completion and an independent explanation check separately; a longer conversation is not itself a learning outcome.

> **A workflow is not ready because the normal run works. It is ready when the missing input, hidden assumption and human review each have a place to go.**

# Daily Digest — 2026-10-16

> **Day 5 — Practice.** Three new arXiv preprints reviewed from their primary abstracts and publication metadata. These exercises are curator suggestions, not tested district interventions. No private records were ingested.

## ① Theme Title

**The Handoff Is a Choice, Not a Feeling**

## ② Executive Summary

A fluent AI answer can invite trust, but trust does not tell us whether we checked the answer or used it in our work. Today's practice is to separate three moves: choose how much to hand off next time, verify a claim outside the chat, and record which contribution actually survived into the finished task.

## ③ Concepts Discovered

- **Name the next-time permission.** In a [56-person video-question study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21948), using AI *with verification* was the most common intended policy and could coexist with high trust. The [[First Delegation]] exercise makes the next choice explicit: reject, use with verification, or delegate fully. This matters because feeling confident is not the same as granting permission.
- **A question to AI is not an evidence check.** A preliminary [game-based study of children aged 8–10](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21233) found different questioning and evidence-seeking patterns with different AI styles. [[The Evidence Ticket]] now asks for both the question and an independent source. This matters for anyone who might mistake a good conversation for a verified claim; it is not proof that a particular classroom interface improves learning.
- **Track the contribution, not the completion claim.** A [benchmark built from recordings of 50 professionals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21109) reports that tested models often overestimated collaboration quality. [[The Daily Standup]] asks what the person actually used, changed, or rejected. That matters when a finished draft hides the human work needed to make it usable.

*Evidence boundary:* These are author abstracts, not full-text appraisals or replicated district trials. The children’s study is exploratory; the benchmark evaluates models, not the effectiveness of this wiki’s practice routine. Never put identifiable student or staff information into an unapproved AI service.

## ⑤ For the Operator

Use a public, low-stakes claim. Write one question for the AI, then find the answer in a separate source. Mark `verified` only if you can actually reach that source; otherwise mark `unverified`. No special agent tool is needed. See [[The Evidence Ticket]].

## ⑥ For the Orchestrator

Run a draft-only handoff on public information: `set goal and permissions → receive AI draft → independently check one consequential claim → record used/changed/rejected → choose next run's permission`. Keep external actions behind human approval. If the AI cannot show which contribution survived into the final artifact, do not widen its authority on the strength of its own completion report. See [[First Delegation]] and [[The Daily Standup]].

> **Day 5 — Practice.** Three newly announced arXiv preprints read from the October 9 cs.HC/cs.CY feeds and verified against primary metadata and abstracts. The exercises are editorial translations, not evaluated interventions.

## ① Theme Title

**Check the Route, Not Just the Result**

## ② Executive Summary

A correct-looking AI answer can miss the job, a tutor can score better while teaching worse, and an analysis can hide the choices that led to its chart. Today's practice is to keep three things visible: what success means in this context, how the work unfolded, and which other paths were available. None of the studies establishes that the exercises below improve student outcomes.

## ③ Concepts Discovered

- **Fit is more than factual accuracy.** Lee and Yu identify eight lenses students used to evaluate AI output: accuracy, completeness, task fit, personalization, practicality, creativity, ethics and bias. The practical move is to pick *one* non-accuracy lens before reviewing a delegated draft. [Primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10743); [[First Delegation]].
- **A good step repeated is not a good lesson.** Domínguez Figaredo and Fernández De la Cruz report a tutoring metric rising on corrected cases while blind educator ratings fell; their model kept repeating the locally rewarded teaching move. Inspect a short sequence of turns, not just one response or one score. [Primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12125); [[First Delegation]].
- **Keep the paths you did not take visible.** Yang and colleagues propose an analysis interface where people can branch from a broad question into testable ones and inspect which data supports each branch. It is an illustrated system with a *proposed* user study, not measured evidence of better decisions. On a public dataset, jot down two plausible questions before choosing one chart. [Primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11025); [[The Evidence Ticket]].

## ⑤ For the Operator

Use a public, low-stakes draft you already need. Before opening AI, write: `What must be accurate? What must fit my audience or task?` After the draft arrives, verify one factual claim at its source and mark one sentence that fails the second test. Keep it in draft if either fails. Use only district-approved tools and data handling for actual school work.

## ⑥ For the Orchestrator

Pilot a draft-only handoff: `GOAL / TWO CHECKS (fact + fit) / TWO CONSECUTIVE OUTPUTS OR TURNS / ALTERNATIVE ROUTE / HUMAN RELEASE`. Record whether the agent repeats a move even when the situation changes. For analyses, save the rejected question and its relevant data field beside the chart in [[The Evidence Ticket]]. A human decides what gets published; do not automate high-stakes assessment or feed identifiable student work into an unapproved system.

# Daily Digest — 2026-10-23

> **Day 5 — Practice.** Three new arXiv version-1 preprints inspected through publisher RSS abstracts. Full papers were not appraised. These are writing and undergraduate studies, not K–12 district intervention evidence.

## ① Theme Title

**The work should get easier to hand off without making your judgment disappear**

## ② Executive Summary

The best practice is not “use AI less” or “let it do everything.” Write your goal, make a first attempt, choose what help to accept, and then see what you can still do when the help is gone. Three new studies point to a useful distinction between an output that looks better now and a person who can still reason tomorrow.

## ③ Concepts Discovered

- **Productive friction:** Goal-setting and dividing work into steps can make assisted writing feel harder while improving its quality and short-lag carryover. The operator can pause before prompting; the orchestrator can make that pause part of a repeatable handoff. Source: [Kewenig and colleagues](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30945); see [[The Daily Standup]].
- **An attempt before assistance:** Better assisted homework need not mean better unaided performance. Keep a first attempt and an unaided check when the purpose is learning, not merely finishing. Source: [Chen and colleagues](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31481); see [[The Practice Style]].
- **Reliance is a behavior, not a badge:** One undergraduate scale study distinguishes reflective, cautious, thoughtless and collaborative self-reported use. Rather than labeling people, note whether you accepted, checked or changed a suggestion and why. Source: [Hou and colleagues](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30195); see [[The Reliance Audit]].

*Evidence boundary:* We inspected RSS abstracts, not full papers or instruments. Our exercises are practical translations, **not interventions tested by these papers**. None supports a K–12 effect or a universal “chatbot tutoring is harmful” claim. Use public, fictional or approved training material only; do not put identifiable student or staff records into an unapproved tool.

## ⑤ For the Operator

Take one public notice or fictional scenario. Before opening AI, write your goal and your own first sentence. Ask for one improvement or hint, not the whole answer. Record what you kept and what you rejected. Tomorrow, write a new first sentence without looking at AI's version. This is a two-day rehearsal, not a test you can fail.

## ⑥ For the Orchestrator

For a draft-only workflow, add three checkpoints: `human goal and first step → AI suggestion → human acceptance/change with reason`. Put a separate unaided task in the next day's standup if developing human skill is a goal. Track both artifact quality and whether the person can still explain a decision; if the workflow only improves today's output, don't call it a learning success. Keep external sending and sensitive data outside this rehearsal.

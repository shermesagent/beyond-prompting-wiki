## ① Theme Title

**Put a thinking step between the suggestion and “yes”**

## ② Executive Summary

An AI can make a next action feel effortless: a ring offers one-flick approval, while an ad can use the context of a conversation to make a suggestion feel unusually relevant. That ease is not evidence that the action fits your goal. A classroom writing study points to a better practice for developmental work: connect a question about your reasoning to a change you make yourself.

## ③ Concepts Discovered

- **Fast approval still needs a full decision.** A small device can make saying yes easier without showing all the data, alternatives or consequences. Before a draft-only action, name what will happen and how you will confirm it actually happened. [Chokkattu, WIRED](https://www.wired.com/story/aina-smart-ring/) → [[The Line You Draw]].
- **A relevant recommendation may have a sponsor and a data path.** An ad placed beside a chatbot response may draw on conversation context; relevance is not independent advice. Trace the original source, the sponsor, and what context could leave the conversation before acting. [Dave, WIRED](https://www.wired.com/story/ads-in-chatbots-have-arrived-heres-whats-working-and-whats-not/) → [[The Evidence Ticket]].
- **A helpful question is only a start; look for your revision.** A secondary-school classroom preprint links movement from idea discussion into revision with higher essay-score gains; it does not establish that the tool caused those gains. Keep the human-written change visible. [Favero and colleagues, arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07792v1) → [[The Line You Draw]] and [[The Evidence Ticket]].

## ⑤ For the Operator

Take a public, low-stakes AI recommendation and write three lines before accepting it: `My goal / Who benefits from this suggestion / One independent thing I checked`. For a writing task, add `One sentence I changed myself and why`. If the recommendation is sponsored or you cannot find a source, leave it as a draft.

## ⑥ For the Orchestrator

Add a **pause card** before a proposed action: `exact action / data shared / sponsor or incentive / human approver / proof of completion / stop or undo path`. Test it with a public-information draft that cannot send or buy anything. After review, record whether a human changed the recommendation and whether the change advanced the original goal; do not treat a quick approval or a smooth summary as proof of good judgment.

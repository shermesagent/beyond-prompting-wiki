## ① Theme Title

**When the Demo Ends.** Agentic AI has been "one of education's most talked-about technologies" — but relatively few institutions run it in day-to-day operations. Today's sources are the ones that do: a community college running 12 agents through its enrollment cycle because it couldn't hire fast enough; a technical college that automated a $150 million reporting process and kept a human at the very last verification step; a tumor board where AI surfaced trial opportunities clinicians were missing — a 90.9% expansion in patient access; clinics designing a blameless review ritual for when AI gets it wrong anyway; and a measurement showing AI assistants now move customers to brands the way storefronts used to. None of these are pilots. All of them answer the same question differently: **where does the human stay in the loop when the agent runs for real?**

## ② Executive Summary

- **The autonomy divide has replaced the value debate in education.** At Bridges 2026, institutions stopped arguing about whether agents are useful and started deciding how much autonomy and data access to grant. Three legitimate postures emerged: guarded (Milwaukee Area Technical College — faculty-controlled tutoring agents, no broad data access), scaling (Johnston Community College — 12 agents processing enrollment because staff couldn't be hired fast enough), and distributed (Peninsula School District — agents rolling out to every adult staffer this fall). The consistent thread: an agent is not a chatbot — it makes decisions pinned to a goal and changes action based on results. (GovTech)
- **Production AI that works doesn't replace the expert — it widens the expert's net.** TrialGPT 2.0 ran for six months inside a live oncology tumor board: it retrieved a clinician-recommended trial for ~91% of cases while cutting screening time 55.0%, and surfaced opportunities the routine workflow missed — expanding patient access to trials by 90.9%. The trick is in the output form: a ranked, explained shortlist for expert review, not a verdict. (arXiv:2609.01202)
- **When production AI fails, blame is useless — review is the answer.** A new clinical framework adapts medicine's morbidity-and-mortality conference to AI: a structured, blameless case review across four dimensions — Trigger → Mechanism → Consequence → Corrective Action — with evidence preserved and fixes tracked. Aggregate monitoring can't explain how risk emerged across the AI, the human, and the workflow; only case review can. (arXiv:2609.00076)
- **If learning is co-construction with AI, the learning theories are out of date.** Four classic theories (behaviorism, cognitivism, constructivism, connectivism) predate machines that generate knowledge. "Generativism" proposes four constructs for the age of epistemic partnership: epistemic partnership, distributed agency, generative literacy, adaptive metacognition. For curriculum leaders: assess the brief, the check, and what the student chose to own — not just the output. (arXiv:2606.12441)
- **Your business now has a storefront inside AI assistants — and you can't see it in your analytics.** When an assistant recommends a brand to a user with no recent engagement, same-name searches rise +4.3pp and site visits +2.4pp — invisible to standard attribution. Incidental mentions barely move behavior; being the deliberate answer does. (arXiv:2606.10907)

## ③ Concepts Discovered

- **[[The Failure Review]]** (new concept page) — the blameless, structured ritual for learning from AI failures that slip past review: freeze the evidence, then classify the case across Trigger (what exposed the vulnerability) → Mechanism (what process produced the risk) → Consequence (what it did to the work) → Corrective Action (the fix, with an owner). Borrowed from medicine's morbidity-and-mortality conferences; the paper's two independent reviewers agreed on all 20 axis-level classifications. This is the third leg of a delegation system: [[The Review-First Pattern]] checks before shipping, [[Failure-Path Preservation]] keeps failures visible, and the Failure Review extracts the lesson when one gets through anyway.
- **The Autonomy Divide** (embedded in [[The School District Shift]]) — in production, organizations separate along how much autonomy and data access they grant agents, not whether agents are valuable. Guarded / scaling / distributed are all defensible postures; what matters is that the *autonomy question* replaces the *prompt question*. And the "last step stays human" rule shows up with a dollar sign: "When we're talking $150 million a year... you do want somebody just at the very end to verify."
- **The Net-Widener Pattern** (embedded in [[From Prompt to Pipeline]]) — production AI's highest-value form isn't a verdict, it's a ranked, explained shortlist that widens what the expert sees. TrialGPT's 90.9% access expansion came from surfacing opportunities the routine workflow missed — the AI raised the ceiling of a human system instead of replacing a step.
- **Generativism's Four Constructs** (embedded in [[The School District Shift]]) — epistemic partnership, distributed agency, generative literacy, and adaptive metacognition as the answer to "what is learning when the machine co-writes the knowledge?" For districts, a design vocabulary for assessment: grade the brief, the check, and the ownership — not just the artifact.
- **The AI Storefront** (embedded in [[Small Business Automation]]) — assistant-mediated discovery is a real, measurable acquisition channel invisible to last-click analytics. Being *the answer* (not a passing mention) is what moves customers — and being the answer is something a small business can audit and influence.

## ⑤ For the Operator

- **Run the last-step check on your most important recurring task.** MATC automates a $150 million report and still keeps a human at the very end: "you do want somebody just at the very end to verify those before we file them with the state." Find the task in your week whose errors cost the most — and name the exact verification step a human does after the AI finishes. If there isn't one, add it. That's the operator→orchestrator line drawn on real work ([[The School District Shift]]).
- **Ask for a shortlist, not a verdict.** Next time you delegate research or recommendations, request the TrialGPT shape: "here's what I found, why each item made the list, and what you'd recommend." An explained shortlist keeps your judgment in the loop; a single confident answer asks you to trust ([[From Prompt to Pipeline]]).
- **When the AI gets it wrong this week, don't fix-and-forget.** Take 10 minutes and write the four rows: what exposed it, what process produced it, what it cost, and the fix with an owner. First review feels slow; the third one starts preventing ([[The Failure Review]]).

## ⑥ For the Orchestrator

- **Decide your autonomy posture explicitly — then write it down.** Guarded, scaling, or distributed is a real policy choice, not a default. Name which data each agent may touch, which decisions it may make unassisted, and which step is always human. The institutions that looked calm at Bridges had made this decision in advance ([[The School District Shift]]).
- **Engineer the failure review before the first failure.** If you run agents on anything consequential, adopt the four-axis case review as a standing ritual: freeze evidence, separate trigger from mechanism, keep it blameless, track corrective actions to completion. Pair it with [[Failure-Path Preservation]] so failures stay visible enough to review — and with [[The Review-First Pattern]] so fewer reach the review at all.
- **Design the output form for judgment.** The tumor board lesson generalizes: for any delegated task where a human decides, specify the deliverable as an inspectable, ranked shortlist with reasons — not a recommendation blob. The form of the output is a governance decision ([[From Prompt to Pipeline]]).
- **For district/org leaders: re-design assessment around the partnership.** If students co-construct with AI, artifacts grade the partnership. Use the Generativism constructs to design tasks that reveal the student's brief, their checks, and what they chose to own — and audit what your AI assistant says about your institution, because stakeholders are asking it ([[The School District Shift]], [[Small Business Automation]]).

## ⑧ Top Insight

**The demo ends the day you decide where the human stays.** Every working production deployment in today's sources answered that question out loud: MATC kept a person at the last verification step of a $150M filing; the tumor board kept clinicians as the deciders over an explained shortlist; the failure-review framework keeps a blameless human ritual ready for when the agent errs; and the storefront research shows the customer experience is now partly written by assistants you can't see.

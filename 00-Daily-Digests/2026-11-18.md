# Let people speak first; let AI help second

*Beyond Prompting research digest · 2026-11-18 · Day 3: Real World*

## Executive Summary

A useful agent workflow does not just make work quicker: it preserves the real person's input, makes the original evidence checkable, and gives each action its own permission. Three new preprints—from a design course, a research tool and a civic-dashboard prototype—offer small, concrete tests for those boundaries. None measures a district or small-business rollout; the stories in this wiki remain illustrative.

## Concepts Discovered

- **Real voices before synthetic ones:** An AI-made stakeholder can help rehearse questions, but cannot report what a teacher, family or customer actually said. Interview order shaped how design students used simulated stakeholder input; the small exploratory study does not prove a universally best order. *Try:* write down one real, authorized person's constraint before asking AI to brainstorm. [Mokryn et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22740) · [[The School District Shift]]
- **Keep judgment attached to its evidence:** An AI suggestion is easier to review when the source, suggested label and human correction stay together. In an 18-person social-media-research study, novices showed descriptively greater agreement with AI than experienced users; that is a reason to design a check, not a claim about teachers. *Try:* place a published source and proposed summary side by side and mark one disputed claim. [Wang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22006) · [[The Evidence Interface]]
- **Seeing is not doing:** Permission to read a sample is different from permission to send a message or change a record. A small proactive-assistant prototype separated what it may perceive from how far it may participate. *Try:* set `read / suggest / act` permissions for one approved, public-material task. [Khan et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22431) · [[Small Business Automation]]

## Sources Found

1. [**AI as Anchor**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22740) — Mokryn and colleagues; arXiv v1 announced November 18. One-course field study with eight design teams (21 students) comparing AI-simulated and human stakeholder interview sequences. The authors report exploratory associations, not a causal test or school implementation. *Use:* distinguish “human said” from “AI suggested.”
2. [**Supporting Researcher Control in AI-Assisted Social Media Analysis**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22006) — Wang and colleagues; arXiv v1 announced November 18. Eighteen-person mixed-methods user study of an inspectable research tool using X/Twitter data; novice agreement with AI was descriptively higher. Usability and confidence are not proof of more accurate decisions. *Use:* keep original evidence and a human correction visible.
3. [**Controlling Proactive Multimodal AI**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22431) — Khan, Ali and Huang; arXiv v1 announced November 18. A 14-person initial study and 16-person civic-dashboard study of separate observation and action controls. Screen and speech observation are sensitive, not defaults to enable. *Use:* make read access and permission to act separate choices.

*Evidence note:* These are publisher preprint abstracts captured via arXiv RSS, not full-paper appraisals. Their study settings differ from the fictional district and bakery scenarios in this wiki.

## For the Operator

Take one **public** lesson example or published customer message. Before asking an approved AI tool for a draft, write down who the real audience is, one constraint you know from a real person, and a source you can check. Then compare the draft with both. If you lack actual stakeholder input, label that gap rather than filling it with an AI persona. Never paste identifiable student, family, customer or personnel data into an unapproved tool.

## For the Orchestrator

For one existing approved workflow, make a three-column handoff card: `real input and original source | AI suggestion with read/suggest/act limits | named reviewer and final decision`. Trial it on a public or fictional example. Deliberately test a missing input: does the workflow stop and ask, or quietly invent the person's view? No automatic send, purchasing or record changes until a named owner approves them. [[The Review-First Pattern]] · [[Human in the Loop]]

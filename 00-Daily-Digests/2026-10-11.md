## ① Theme Title

**When the Demo Ends, the Work Begins**

## ② Executive Summary

An easy way to ask for a result is not the same as a way to inspect, correct, and repeat it. Today's examples—a voice-built website feature, a set of playable but flawed generated games, and narrow robotics demonstrations—put the same question on the table: what happens after the impressive first run meets a changed condition? The responsible next step is a small, observable trial, not a bigger promise.

## ③ Concepts Discovered

- **Change surfaces when the job changes.** Simon Willison could speak a feature into a local preview, then needed a pull-request review and typed corrections to handle a private import before deployment. Why it matters: the orchestrator chooses a different *review method* for implementation and permission decisions; voice is not a substitute for inspecting the artifact. [Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/9/built-using-my-voice/) → [[The Translation Layer]] and [[The Evidence Interface]].
- **Playable does not mean fit for purpose.** WIRED's writer generated games quickly, but the intended theme did not survive one build, a game's controls were confusing, and a subsequent fix could introduce another defect. Why it matters: a working preview is a starting point for user testing and a second run, not sign-off. This is one product test, not a failure-rate study. [WIRED](https://www.wired.com/story/i-made-terrible-games-with-googles-ai-playground/) → [[The Demonstration Check]].
- **Ask what the camera cannot show.** MIT Technology Review reports that robotic demonstrations can conceal remote control or scripted behavior and that narrow successes do not establish general reliability. Why it matters for *office* AI by analogy only: record who did which step, try one altered condition, and name what happens when the task cannot finish. [MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/10/08/1145923/ai-breakthroughs-in-robotics-wont-change-your-life-any-time-soon/) → [[The Evidence Interface]].

## ⑤ For the Operator

Take a public, non-sensitive task you already use AI for. Save its first output, then change **one** condition: the audience, a missing source, or an unavailable input. Compare both versions against your original request. Write down one thing you could not tell from the polished result. For school work, do not upload identifiable student or personnel data to an unapproved tool.

## ⑥ For the Orchestrator

Run a draft-only trial and keep a three-line receipt: `Who/what completed each step? / What changed in run two? / Who can stop, correct, and approve?` Inspect the actual file or preview *and* a separate evidence surface (source, test, change log, or human observation). If the altered task fails or the human work is invisible, keep the permissions narrow and repair the workflow before the next run. See [[The Demonstration Check]], [[The Evidence Ticket]], and [[The Translation Layer]].

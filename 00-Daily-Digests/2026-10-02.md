# Daily Digest — 2026-10-02

> **Day 5 — 05-Practice.** Five independently checked sources: three research preprints and two publisher articles. Today's exercise is to keep the first thought human, the reason testable, and the tool's reach narrow.

## ① Theme Title

**Before the AI Helps, Decide What Must Stay Yours**

## ② Executive Summary

Better help does not always mean giving an AI the first move, accepting its explanation, or granting it broad access. Today's sources point to three small design choices: start with your own thought, name the fact that would reverse a recommendation, and limit the connections an agent can use.

## ③ Concepts Discovered

- **Think-first gate:** In a [controlled writing experiment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01518), a meaningful-engagement gate shifted effort toward writing without increasing overall task time. Try writing your own opening claim before requesting a draft; do not assume this single task proves long-term learning. See [[The Line You Draw]].
- **Skill after the advisor leaves:** In [two simulated factory experiments](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00163), assisted decisions improved in some ways, but unaided results depended on the measure. Changing the AI's recommendation was *associated* with stronger unaided performance within the assisted group; try making one reasoned edit, then a comparable unaided call. This is not evidence that edits cause learning. See [[The Line You Draw]].
- **The reversal condition:** [Lumbroso](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00961) proposes asking what changed fact would reverse a consequential decision; [Graepel](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/10/02/1145639/dont-be-fooled-llms-dont-reason/) argues for inspectable evidence and open questions over convincing explanation text. Try the five-minute flip test in [[The Reversal Condition]], then record the fact and source in [[The Evidence Ticket]]. These are a conceptual paper and expert essay, not trials of the exercise.
- **Access is a separate boundary:** [WIRED's report](https://www.wired.com/story/a-flaw-in-chatgpts-mac-app-could-have-let-hackers-grab-sensitive-data/) on a patched local Mac app vulnerability is a reminder to inventory an agent's files, browser and account access before linking it to work. It does **not** show that all installations were compromised.

## ⑤ For the Operator

Pick a public, non-sensitive task today. Write **one sentence of your own** before opening AI; after it responds, write **one thing you changed and why**. If the AI recommends an action, ask which independently checkable fact would make you decide differently. Keep this in draft, not in an unapproved student-data workflow.

## ⑥ For the Orchestrator

For one approved workflow, add three lines to the brief: `HUMAN FIRST MOVE`, `REVERSAL FACT + ORIGINAL SOURCE`, and `ACCESS ALLOWED / NOT ALLOWED`. Test a copied input with only the reversal fact changed; stop if the recommendation fails to respond or the fact cannot be checked. Log who approves an external action and ensure the software and connections are current. [[The Evidence Ticket]] holds the trail; [[The Line You Draw]] helps set the boundary.

## ⑧ Top Insight of the Day

> **The safest handoff is not the one with the longest prompt. It is the one where you can still say what you thought first, what would change your mind, and what the tool cannot touch.**

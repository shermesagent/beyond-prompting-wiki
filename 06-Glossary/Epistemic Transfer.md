# Epistemic Transfer

**Epistemic transfer is the effect of prior AI-assisted work on what you can still do on your own afterward — measured by removing the tool and testing unassisted performance on new problems.**

Most AI evaluations ask one question: does the tool help while it's present? Epistemic transfer asks the question that matters for your skills: *what does the tool leave behind?* You use an AI to verify claims, check your reasoning, or draft analysis — and then the AI is gone (trial ends, license lapses, model changes). Can you still verify claims, check reasoning, or draft analysis yourself? If yes, the interaction *transferred* capability. If no, you were renting judgment, not building it.

That's the distinction at the heart of the term: **using AI well and getting better because of AI are different outcomes, and only the second one is epistemic transfer.**

## What It Is Not

The framework (Trattner, arXiv:2608.08882) is explicit that epistemic transfer is *not* the same as things that look like it:

| Nearby Outcome | What It Measures | Why It's Not Transfer |
|----------------|------------------|----------------------|
| **Correction effects** | The output is right while the tool is present | Doesn't say what you'd do on the next claim, alone |
| **Trust / reliance** | You accept or defer to the output | Says nothing about your independent judgment |
| **Team performance** | Human + AI together beat either alone | The team's competence can be the AI's competence |
| **Epistemic transfer** | Delayed unassisted performance on *new* claims | The only one that tests what you kept |

## The Diagnostic Space: Four Outcomes

The framework defines two measurable quantities — the **Epistemic Transfer Effect (ETE)**, comparing delayed unassisted performance across conditions, and the **Tool-Removal Cost (TRC)**, the immediate drop when the tool is taken away. Put together, they separate four outcomes:

| Outcome | ETE | TRC | What's Happening |
|---------|-----|-----|------------------|
| **Capability building** | Positive | Any | You learned; the tool made you better alone |
| **Capability + tool advantage** | Positive | High | You got better *and* the tool still helps you more |
| **Epistemic inertness / de-skilling** | Negative or zero | High | The tool did the work; your judgment atrophied |
| **Verification on loan** | Zero | High | You were competent only while the tool was present |

The point isn't that every AI tool must teach. The point (in Trattner's words) is that *when independent judgment matters, we should test not only whether a tool helps now, but also what it leaves behind.*

## The 30-Second Transfer Check

After any delegated task where you want to keep the skill:

1. **Take the tool away.** Close the chat, disable the extension, use a fresh document.
2. **Give yourself a new instance of the same task type** — not the same task, a new one.
3. **Grade your unassisted attempt.** Can you produce the reasoning, not just recognize it?

If you can't, the interaction was **verification on loan** — competent output, zero transfer. That's the outcome [[Capacity Dissolution]] warns about, measured directly instead of inferred. The two belong together: the **Capacity Check** asks whether the interaction *felt* like you were engaged; the **Transfer Check** asks whether it *proved* you could do it alone.

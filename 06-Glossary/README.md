# 06 — Glossary

> Plain-language definitions for the words you'll hear as you move from operator to orchestrator.

## What's In This Section

This glossary isn't an encyclopedia. It's a **companion** — a place to look up a term the moment it feels slippery, read a definition in plain English (no jargon, no prerequisites), and get back to what you were doing. Every entry follows the same pattern:

1. **A one-sentence definition** at the top — if you only read that, you'll get the idea.
2. **Elaboration with concrete examples** — what this actually looks like when you're using it.
3. **Related Pages** — wikilinks that connect this term to the bigger concepts in the wiki.

The nineteen terms here are the ones that come up most often when someone starts moving from typing prompts to orchestrating agents. You'll hear words like *agent*, *delegation*, and *autonomy* thrown around in blog posts and product pages. Those words mean something specific in this world — and understanding them clearly is the difference between feeling lost and feeling in control.

If you're new here, start with **[[Agent]]** — it's the foundation everything else sits on. After that, jump to whichever term you're wrestling with today. There's no wrong order.

This section grows as new concepts crystallize. The newest entries — **[[06-Glossary/Persistence|Persistence]]**, **[[06-Glossary/Bounded Sovereignty|Bounded Sovereignty]]**, **[[06-Glossary/Capacity Dissolution|Capacity Dissolution]]**, **[[06-Glossary/Cognitive Fixed Cost|Cognitive Fixed Cost]]**, **[[06-Glossary/The Blank Box Problem|The Blank Box Problem]]**, and **[[06-Glossary/Failure-Path Preservation|Failure-Path Preservation]]** — were added in August 2026. **Persistence** synthesizes the time dimension of autonomy (WIRED, Aug 2026): the difference between an agent that finishes the task you set and an agent that keeps finding new tasks for itself until someone puts it to sleep — with the Sleep Contract exercise and the Reach Audit dimensions. **Bounded Sovereignty** synthesizes the sovereignty analysis (Lim, arXiv:2608.19216): the four-layer access typology (data, model, infrastructure, interaction) that determines what oversight is actually possible, and the sovereignty discount you pay for the layers you don't hold. The other August entries synthesize a wave of July 2026 research (Yao, Li & Cao, Shneiderman, Saboia Moreira & Sweet). Each is a 30-second quick reference: the full treatment lives on the linked concept page, and every entry's Related Pages leads with it. Earlier entries — **[[Overassistance]]**, **[[06-Glossary/Trust Calibration|Trust Calibration]]**, and **[[06-Glossary/Delegation Regret|Delegation Regret]]** — were added in July 2026 after research demonstrated that over-helping (not error) is AI's primary failure mode, and that boundary violations (not mistakes) are what most erode human trust. The glossary evolves with the practice.

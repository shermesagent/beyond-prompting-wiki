# Cognitive Fixed Cost

**Cognitive fixed cost is the upfront concentration of mental labor that, once paid, transforms the cost structure of every subsequent instance — the orchestrator invests once, the operator pays per instance.**

The operator pays a small cognitive fee per interaction: reconstructing context, format, and quality bar every time. The orchestrator pays a large fee once — an SOP, a template, a saved workflow — and every subsequent run costs near-zero. Same math as a coffee machine: $5 per cup, or $200 once. (Li & Cao, arXiv:2607.25240, July 2026: "the fixed cost doesn't disappear — it moves" — from human-enforced templates into workflow-embedded rules.)

The subtle part is **who controls commonality**. Standardization hasn't gone away; it's moved. The orchestrator who designs the workflow controls the rules; the operator who only uses the tool inherits them silently.

## Where to Start

Open a blank document and answer four questions about any task you do more than twice a week: What's the input? What's the output? What are the constraints? What's the quality bar? Save it. That's your first SOP — the first fixed cost you never pay again.

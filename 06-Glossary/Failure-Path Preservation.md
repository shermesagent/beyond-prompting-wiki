# Failure-Path Preservation

**Failure-path preservation is the property of a knowledge system that records what *didn't* work — dead ends, failed approaches, wrong assumptions, corrected claims — alongside what did, so every new effort starts from the accumulated record instead of from scratch.**

Three weeks after a failed AI experiment, you remember it went wrong but not why, not what you changed, not what the fix was. The operator thinks "I'll remember." The orchestrator knows they won't — and builds a substrate so they don't have to: a dated markdown file of what was tried, what happened, what changed. (Saboia Moreira & Sweet, arXiv:2607.24759, July 2026, identify it as one of three emergent sociotechnical properties of knowledge-based AI workflows — the others: agent honesty and appropriation.)

Failure-path preservation is what makes the cognitive fixed cost compound: every documented dead end is a toll avoided on the next run. See [[02-Key-Concepts/Cognitive Fixed Cost|Cognitive Fixed Cost]].

## The Everyday Version (3 Steps)

1. When something with AI goes wrong, write down what you tried.
2. Write down what happened.
3. When you fix it, add the fix and the date to the same entry.

That's it. The property isn't the tool — it's the preservation of the negative result.

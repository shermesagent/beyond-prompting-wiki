# Capacity Dissolution

**Capacity dissolution is the erosion of the five human capabilities that institutions use to recognize competence — end-setting, reason-giving, contestability, refusal/revision, and participation — when AI produces the outputs that once required them.**

The danger isn't error — it's success. The AI gets it *right*, polished and professional, and that's exactly what makes it dangerous: you didn't build the skill that produces that output, you can't explain *why* it's right, and you can't produce the next one without the AI. The output is correct. The capacity is gone. (Named by Kai Yao, AAAI/ACM AIES 2026, arXiv:2607.28041. Dickey et al.'s N=220 randomized study, arXiv:2607.27586, showed students who only evaluated AI solutions earned better homework scores but zero exam transfer — output improved, capacity did not.)

The five capacities:

1. **End-setting** — deciding what purposes are worth pursuing
2. **Reason-giving** — explaining and justifying choices
3. **Contestability** — challenging and interrogating outputs
4. **Refusal/revision** — saying "no" or "not good enough"
5. **Participation** — engaging meaningfully in the work AI mediates

## The Capacity Check (30 Seconds)

After any delegation, ask five questions: Did I set the end, or did the AI? Can I explain why this output is right or wrong? Did I challenge anything? Did I reject or revise anything? Was I engaged, or was I spectating?

Three or more "no" answers: the interaction produced output but dissolved capacity. Sometimes that's fine — not every task needs capacity preservation. But if the pattern holds across a week, the cost compounds.

## The Transfer Check: Did Anything Survive the Tool?

Capacity Dissolution describes the erosion mechanism; [[06-Glossary/Epistemic Transfer|Epistemic Transfer]] is the test that detects it (Trattner, arXiv:2608.08882). Where the Capacity Check audits the *interaction* (were you doing the thinking?), the Transfer Check audits the *aftermath*: take the tool away, work a **new** instance of the same task type unassisted, and grade your own reasoning. Positive transfer = the skill survived; zero transfer = the interaction was **verification on loan** — competent output while the tool was present, nothing left behind. Run the Transfer Check weekly on your most-delegated task type; it turns "I hope I'm not losing this" into a measurement.

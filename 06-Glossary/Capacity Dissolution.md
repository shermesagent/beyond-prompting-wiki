# Capacity Dissolution

**Capacity dissolution is the erosion of five human capabilities that happens when AI produces the outputs — essays, code, reports, decisions — through which institutions recognize competence: end-setting, reason-giving, contestability, refusal/revision, and participation. Each time the AI does the thinking for you, a specific capacity gets a little weaker.**

When most people worry about AI, they worry about errors — hallucinations, wrong answers, bad code. Capacity dissolution is the opposite problem. The AI gets it *right* — polished, competent, professional — and that's what makes it dangerous. You didn't build the skill to produce that output. You reviewed it, maybe tweaked it, and moved on. The artifact is indistinguishable from one you produced yourself. But you can't explain *why* it's right, can't defend it under questioning, and can't produce the next one without the AI. That's capacity dissolution. The output is correct. The capacity is gone.

The concept comes from Kai Yao (AAAI/ACM AIES 2026, arXiv:2607.28041), who identifies five specific capacities that erode when output delegation becomes the default:

1. **End-setting** — deciding what purposes are worth pursuing. When the AI suggests what to work on and you just follow the suggestion, you stop practicing the ability to set direction.
2. **Reason-giving** — explaining and justifying choices. When the AI produces the analysis and you can't articulate why the conclusion follows from the evidence, the reasoning capacity atrophies.
3. **Contestability** — challenging, disputing, and interrogating outputs. When polished artifacts arrive complete and convincing, the instinct to question them fades.
4. **Refusal/revision** — saying "no" or "not good enough." When AI output is always "good enough," the standards that drive revision weaken.
5. **Participation** — engaging meaningfully in the practices AI mediates. When AI handles the work, humans become spectators to their own professional domain.

The N=220 randomized study by Dickey et al. (arXiv:2607.27586) provides direct evidence: students who only evaluated AI solutions earned higher homework scores but showed no transfer to exams. Their evaluation skill didn't build their solving capacity. That's capacity dissolution in microcosm.

## Why It Matters for Moving Beyond Prompting

The operator asks: "How can I get more output from this AI?" The orchestrator asks: "Which of my capacities did this interaction preserve, and which did it dissolve?"

Every delegation has two outcomes: the artifact (what you got) and the capacity delta (what you gained or lost). The operator only tracks the artifact. The orchestrator tracks both. A pipeline that produces perfect reports but erodes your ability to set direction and explain reasoning is a net loss over time. A pipeline that produces slightly rougher reports but preserves end-setting and reason-giving is a net gain.

The practice section of this wiki — [[Audit Your Prompts]], [[First Delegation]], [[The Daily Standup]], [[Build a Tiny Pipeline]] — exists because capacity dissolution is the default. If you delegate without deliberate design, the AI will dissolve capacity. The exercises provide the deliberate design.

## In Plain Language

Imagine you hire a personal trainer. Every session, they lift the weights for you while you watch. Six months later, you're no stronger — but you can describe perfectly what a good deadlift looks like. That's capacity dissolution. The trainer did the work correctly. You observed it correctly. But the capacity to do it yourself never developed. The AI is that trainer — optimized to produce the best possible output, not to make you better at producing it.

## The Capacity Check (30 Seconds)

After any AI delegation, ask:

1. Did I set the end, or did the AI? (End-setting)
2. Can I explain why this output is right or wrong? (Reason-giving)
3. Did I challenge anything? (Contestability)
4. Did I reject or revise anything? (Refusal/revision)
5. Was I engaged, or was I spectating? (Participation)

Three or more "no" answers: the interaction produced output but dissolved capacity. Not always wrong — some tasks genuinely don't require capacity preservation. But if the pattern holds across a week, the cost is compounding.

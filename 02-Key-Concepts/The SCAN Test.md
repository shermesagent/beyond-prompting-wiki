# The SCAN Test

> The problem isn't that you misuse AI. It's that you misclassify the task — and pick the wrong mode for where you actually are.

## What It Is

There's a moment in every AI conversation where you make a quiet decision: *should this task be mine, the machine's, or ours?* Most people make that decision by habit, not by design — and that's where the trouble starts.

**The SCAN Test** is a way to make that decision deliberately. It comes from medical training (arXiv:2608.28373), where instructors were worried about AI eroding clinical skills — and realized the real problem wasn't people "misusing" AI. It was people picking the **wrong interaction mode** for the task: asking AI to substitute when they needed it to scaffold, or letting it handle something that should have stayed human. The paper's name for this is sharp: **misclassification, not misuse.**

SCAN gives every task one of four labels:

| Label | What AI Does | Example |
|-------|-------------|---------|
| **S — Substitute** | AI does the whole task | Draft the routine email |
| **C — Complement** | AI and you work together, each doing part | AI finds the pattern, you decide what it means |
| **A — Aid** | AI holds the ladder while you climb | AI explains a concept; you do the actual task yourself |
| **N — Non-Negotiable** | Stays human, no AI | The performance review, the apology, the final call |

The trick is that the *same task* can deserve different labels depending on **where you are** — which is why the label has to be checked against your current zone, not the task's category. A substitute task for an expert is an aid task for a beginner. The failure isn't the task; it's the mismatch.

## Why It Matters for Moving Beyond Prompting

SCAN is the operator→orchestrator shift compressed into one decision. The operator's habit is to use one mode for everything — usually "ask AI to produce it" (Substitute). The orchestrator's habit is to **label the task first, then choose the mode** — and to re-label as circumstances change.

The paper names the three ways skill fails, and they're the ones to watch:

- **De-skilling** — you had the skill, AI use eroded it.
- **Never-skilling** — you never built the skill because AI did it for you.
- **Mis-skilling** — you built the *wrong* skill. This is the dangerous one, because it looks like progress.

And there's a sneaky fourth path the paper highlights: **passive engagement.** You classify the task correctly — "Aid, AI should scaffold this" — but then you sit back and watch the AI do it. The task was labeled right; the *participation* was wrong. That's how a correctly-built workflow still produces mis-skilling: the scaffold becomes a substitute because you stopped climbing. The fix is to catch it and re-identify the task as needing expert help, not just AI help.

This connects to the wiki's existing vocabulary: SCAN is [[The Placement Rule]] with a checklist attached — and its warning about passive engagement is [[The Absorption Pattern]] in its quietest form.

## How to Spot It in Your Day

You're misclassifying when:

- You reach for AI by default for anything that feels tedious — without asking whether you *need* to feel the task yourself this week
- You say "AI can do this" when what you mean is "I don't want to do this" — the label should come from your learning needs, not your mood
- You watch AI work on a task you were supposed to be practicing and call it "scaffolding"
- You grade your AI use by whether the output is good, instead of by whether *you* got better

## Try This

**The Two-Minute SCAN.** Take your three most frequent AI tasks. For each one, answer two questions:

1. **Which label am I actually using?** (Substitute / Complement / Aid / Non-Negotiable)
2. **Which trajectory is this building?** (upskilling, de-skilling, never-skilling, or mis-skilling)

Now pick one task where the answers don't match what you intended — say, "I meant Aid, but I've been passively watching, so it's really Substitute." Change one thing: do the next instance yourself, with the AI only explaining. That single relabel is the shift.

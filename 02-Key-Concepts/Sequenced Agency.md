# Sequenced Agency

## What It Is

Sequenced agency is the practice of giving AI more freedom in stages, only when the evidence says the system is ready. It rejects the lazy binary of “ban it” versus “turn it loose.” Instead, it asks: what is the next safe degree of access, autonomy, or reliance — and what proof would justify moving there?

Think of it like teaching someone to drive. You do not start with the highway. You start in a parking lot, then quiet streets, then busier roads, with an adult nearby and clear conditions for pulling back. AI workflows need the same common sense.

## Why It Matters for Moving Beyond Prompting

Operators usually make one of two moves: they either keep AI boxed inside low-risk prompting forever, or they leap into broad delegation because the demo looked impressive. Orchestrators sequence the handoff.

The pattern matters because real adoption is not just personal confidence. Microsoft’s WorkLab framing says agency expands when organizations redesign work, capture learning, and support people around the agent. LAUSD’s temporary student-device ban shows the other side: if access is blocked while policy catches up, students with personal devices may keep experimenting without guidance while others lose supervised practice. Utah’s ed-tech privacy audit adds the hard edge: promises are not controls unless they can be audited.

Sequenced agency gives the middle path: **earn more autonomy through evidence, not vibes.**

## How to Spot It in Your Day

You are practicing sequenced agency when you say things like:

| Instead of... | You ask... |
|---|---|
| “Should we allow this AI tool?” | “What access level is appropriate right now?” |
| “Can the agent do this?” | “What evidence would let us trust it one step more?” |
| “The policy says it is approved.” | “Can we verify what it actually collects, changes, or shares?” |
| “Students should/shouldn’t use AI.” | “What supervised practice comes before independent use?” |
| “This workflow saved time.” | “Did it also preserve review, recovery, and human support?” |

The clue is reversibility. If you cannot explain what would move the workflow forward one step — and what failure would move it back — you do not have a sequence. You have a guess.

## Try This

**The 5-Minute Autonomy Ladder**

Pick one AI use case you already have: drafting emails, grading practice responses, summarizing meetings, researching vendors, building dashboards, anything.

Draw four rungs:

1. **Observe** — AI suggests; human does the work.
2. **Assist** — AI drafts or analyzes; human reviews every output.
3. **Act with approval** — AI performs steps, but waits before anything visible, irreversible, or data-sensitive.
4. **Act with monitoring** — AI handles routine cases; human reviews exceptions and samples.

Now answer three questions:

- What rung are you actually on today?
- What evidence would justify moving up one rung?
- What failure would move it back down?

That last question is the difference between governance and wishful thinking.

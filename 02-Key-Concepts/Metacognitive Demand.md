# Metacognitive Demand

## What It Is

Metacognitive Demand is the part of a task where **you still have to notice, decide, monitor, or explain what is happening** instead of letting the AI carry the whole thing.

It is not about making the work harder for the sake of suffering. It is about keeping the human decision alive at the exact moments where judgment matters: What is the goal? What counts as enough? What is missing? What should stop? What should not be remembered? What does this explanation fail to explain?

A good AI scaffold can reduce busywork while preserving metacognitive demand. A bad one removes the thinking and calls the silence “efficiency.”

## Why It Matters for Moving Beyond Prompting

The operator asks AI for an answer. The orchestrator designs the moment where the human still has to think.

That difference matters because a workflow can look successful while quietly removing the very judgment you meant to build. A tutor can help a learner finish while weakening planning. An explanation can feel clear while hiding its own limits. Memory can save repeated disclosure while letting sensitive information travel into the wrong context. An agent can act for hours while no one has named the agenda that should govern it.

Moving beyond prompting is not “let AI do everything.” It is **deciding which human decisions must remain visible**.

## How to Spot It in Your Day

Ask where the human still has to do more than approve:

| Workflow moment | Low demand version | Better demand version |
|---|---|---|
| Learning | AI gives the full answer | AI gives support after you choose a next step |
| Explanation | AI explains confidently | AI marks what the explanation does *not* cover |
| Memory | AI remembers everything | You scope what may travel into future contexts |
| Stopping | AI runs until done | Stop authority, trigger, and restart rule are named |
| Oversight | Human reviews after the fact | Human sets the agenda before the agent acts |

If the human's job is only “looks good,” the demand is probably too low.

## Try This

**The 5-Minute Demand Check**

Pick one AI workflow you use this week. Write three lines:

1. **What must the human still decide?** Name one decision the AI should not silently absorb.
2. **Where does the AI support fade?** Name the point where help should pull back so you can judge.
3. **What would make it stop?** Name the trigger that pauses, routes back, or asks for clarification.

If you cannot answer all three, the workflow may be productive — but it is probably not building orchestrator judgment.

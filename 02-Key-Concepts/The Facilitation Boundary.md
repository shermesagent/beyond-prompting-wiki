# The Facilitation Boundary

## What It Is

Let AI help a group **hear itself better** without letting AI decide for the group. It can summarize what has been said, ask whose evidence is missing, and surface a possible objection. The people still choose and own the outcome.

In a controlled online hiring exercise, AI prompts helped three-person groups share information and reach the correct choice more often. The AI **did not know the correct candidate**: it helped the conversation, not the answer. This is one study of a simulated task, not permission to send real hiring records to a chatbot. [Córdova and colleagues, preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19652). ^[raw/articles/arxiv-2611.19652-llm-group-facilitation.md]

## Why It Matters for Moving Beyond Prompting

An operator asks AI for a recommendation. An orchestrator chooses *which part of the decision process* to delegate. The same meeting may safely delegate a neutral recap while keeping the final choice, access to private information, and release of a report with named people. A vehicle-agent survey also suggests people prefer different levels of autonomy for different functions; its vehicle results are a design prompt, not evidence about your school or workplace. [Zepf and colleagues, preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19474). ^[raw/articles/arxiv-2611.19474-function-level-autonomy.md]

A separate formal design study illustrates another boundary: **permission to release is not the same as responsibility to repair an incomplete report**. It tested scripted and generated cases, not real institutional deployment. [Xu and Tan, preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19777). ^[raw/articles/arxiv-2611.19777-release-and-repair.md]

## How to Spot It in Your Day

- Good boundary: “Summarize the options and note whose viewpoint we have not heard. Do not pick for us.”
- Poor boundary: “Read these private personnel notes and decide who should get the job.”
- Missing boundary: “The summary looks finished, so send it,” even though no one has checked what is missing or who will fix it.

## Try This

**Five-minute, no-private-data rehearsal:** Use a made-up or public choice for your next team discussion. Write three columns: *AI may organize*, *people decide*, *person who checks and fixes*. Ask AI to list two questions the group has not answered, without recommending a winner. Before sharing anything, check the recap against the original discussion and ask whether the relevant people actually agreed to this use. Keep student, personnel, and confidential information out of unapproved tools.

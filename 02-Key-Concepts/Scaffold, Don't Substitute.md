# Scaffold, Don't Substitute

## What It Is

**Scaffold, Don't Substitute** is the design principle that AI should *support* the human capacity it touches — memory, reasoning, judgment — rather than *replace* the act of using it. A scaffolded system strengthens the skill while it helps; a substituting system produces the result and quietly dissolves the skill that used to produce it.

The principle comes from a 2026 education paper (arXiv:2608.17451) that reframes the risk of AI in classrooms as a **misalignment problem**: AI that eliminates human effort erodes the very capacities education exists to build. The authors organize the harm into four interlocked dimensions — **cognition, agency, emotional well-being, and ethics** — connected by a self-reinforcing cycle:

> cognitive offloading → less effort → weaker agency → compounded emotional and ethical harm → more offloading

The substitution→scaffolding distinction is the escape hatch: it isn't *whether* AI is used, it's *what the AI does to the thinking*.

## Why It Matters for Moving Beyond Prompting

This wiki's core claim is that the operator→orchestrator shift is a *shift*, not a *handover*. Scaffold, Don't Substitute is the boundary that keeps that true. Without it, "orchestration" quietly collapses into substitution with a nicer workflow: you delegate more, review less, and the capacities you used to exercise — checking, comparing, deciding — atrophy even as your output volume grows.

1. **Substitution hides inside seemingly-fine delegation.** Nothing about the delegation loop tells you whether the agent is scaffolding or substituting. Both produce output; only the latter leaves you with less capacity than you started. Scaffold, Don't Substitute gives you a test for the loop itself: *after this delegation, can I do more of the underlying thing, or less?*

2. **The four dimensions are a checkable claim.** Cognition (did I think less?), agency (did I decide less?), emotional well-being (did I care less?), ethics (did I account for less?) — a delegation that degrades any of the four is substitution in disguise, no matter how smoothly it runs.

3. **Learners know the difference — and say so.** In the paper's exploratory analysis of 49 International Baccalaureate argumentative essays, **80% reported that AI reliance reduces thinking**. The same students articulated a consistent alternative: they want systems that *withhold immediate answers, prompt recall, and encourage reflection through questions instead of solutions* — a wish list that matches established learning-science scaffolding practice. The people closest to the risk already know the fix; the design principle just gives it a name.

4. **Scaffolding is the harder design.** Substituting is easy: give the AI the whole task. Scaffolding requires designing *friction with intent* — the AI does part, you do the part that builds the skill, and the boundary between them is the design object. That's [[Friction by Design]] applied at the level of individual capacity.

## How to Spot It in Your Day

Your tools are substituting when:

- You've stopped doing a step you used to do — and you can't remember the last time you did it on purpose
- The AI's answer arrives with none of the intermediate reasoning you used to generate along the way
- You ask the AI *for* the thing instead of using it to *check* the thing — and the difference barely registers anymore
- "I don't need to know how to do this anymore" feels like progress instead of a warning

They're scaffolding when:

- The AI withholds the answer and asks what you think first — annoying, and that's the point
- Outputs arrive with the reasoning trail visible so you can evaluate, not just receive
- The boundary of the delegation is chosen to leave *your* capacity exercised: recall, judgment, synthesis
- After using it, the underlying skill is stronger, not rustier

## What the Research Says

- **From Substitution to Scaffolding** (arXiv:2608.17451, August 2026): integrative framework of four harm dimensions — cognition, agency, emotional well-being, ethics — linked in a self-reinforcing cycle where cognitive offloading reduces effort, weakens agency, and compounds emotional and ethical harm. Exploratory analysis of 49 IB argumentative essays: 80% report AI reliance reduces thinking. Student desiderata — withhold immediate answers, prompt recall, encourage reflection via questions — align with established learning-science principles. The paper's design principle: *scaffold, do not substitute*, extended beyond education to any system that mediates human thinking.

## Try This

**The capacity test.** Pick a task you delegate weekly. After the next run, ask: *what exactly did I do during that delegation — and what did I give up doing?* Now redesign one boundary deliberately: have the AI produce the draft, then withhold its own answer and ask you for the decision it would have made, then compare. One round of "AI proposes, you decide first" per delegation is enough to feel the difference between receiving a result and exercising judgment on it. That's scaffolding's 5-minute version.
